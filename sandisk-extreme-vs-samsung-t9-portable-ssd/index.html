Head-to-head portable SSD comparison

SanDisk Extreme vs Samsung T9

SanDisk Extreme Portable SSD and Samsung T9 are both portable SSDs for laptop backups, creator files, travel kits, and everyday project storage, but they sit a speed class apart. SanDisk is the rugged USB 3.2 Gen 2 drive with an IP65 rating, a carabiner loop, and a USB-A adapter in the box. Samsung T9 is the faster USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 drive, with up to 2,000MB/s read speed, hardware encryption, Samsung software, and both USB-C and USB-A cables included.

Buyer guide

Choose by the port, then by the rating.

These drives are a speed class apart, so the first question is whether your devices can use Gen 2x2 at all. After that, the IP rating, carry setup, and box contents decide the better fit.

SanDisk Extreme

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want an integrated loop for clipping the drive inside a camera bag, tech pouch, or travel organizer.
  • You still use USB-A computers often enough that the included USB-C to USB-A adapter matters.
  • You value the published IP65 dust and water resistance for outdoor shoots, commuting, or messy travel bags.
Skip this if
  • You regularly move very large video or project folders and have a confirmed Gen 2x2 host for T9’s higher ceiling.
  • You want hardware encryption and Samsung Magician in the ownership flow.
  • You want both USB-C and USB-A cables included rather than a cable plus an adapter.
Samsung T9

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • Your main computer supports USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 and you want the faster portable SSD for large files.
  • You want both USB Type-C to C and USB Type-C to A cables included, as listed in Samsung’s T9 data sheet.
  • You use Samsung Portable SSD software, Samsung Magician, password protection, and hardware encryption across your storage setup.
Skip this if
  • You need a published dust and water resistance rating for outdoor shoots, commuting, or messy travel bags.
  • You want the smaller, loop-equipped drive for clipping inside a camera bag or travel pouch.
  • Your USB-C ports are Gen 2 only, so T9 would run at the same ceiling as the SanDisk.
20Gb/s USB is the gatekeeper

The advertised top speeds require USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 host support. Many Thunderbolt and USB-C ports do not automatically mean 20Gb/s Gen 2x2 performance, so check your actual port specs before paying for speed.

The IP rating is one-sided here

SanDisk publishes IP65 dust and water resistance. Samsung’s T9 data sheet lists drop resistance up to 3 m but no IP rating, so field moisture exposure is a SanDisk argument, not a shared spec.

Cable and adapter setups

Samsung lists both C-to-C and C-to-A cables in the T9 box. SanDisk includes a C-to-C cable plus a C-to-A adapter. Both reach older USB-A machines; the difference is cable versus adapter, not compatibility.

Before you buy

Check your host, your cable, and your file workflow.

  • Confirm your computer or dock specifically supports USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 if top speed matters.
  • For camera recording, verify your exact camera model, format, cable, and media requirements before relying on any external SSD.
  • Use a real backup plan. A rugged portable SSD is still a single drive that can be lost, corrupted, or damaged.
  • Expect some formatted capacity loss because manufacturers list storage using decimal GB/TB definitions.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which portable SSD is better for most people?

Samsung T9 is the better default if your computer supports USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 and you want up to 2,000MB/s read speed, hardware encryption, Samsung software, and both cable types in the box. SanDisk Extreme is better if the published IP65 rating, carabiner loop, and included USB-A adapter matter more.

Do both drives need a special port for full speed?

No. Only T9 does: its top speeds require a USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 host. SanDisk Extreme is a USB 3.2 Gen 2 drive, so its up to 1,050MB/s read and 1,000MB/s write ceiling applies on the much more common Gen 2 ports. On a Gen 2-only computer, T9 runs at Gen 2 speeds too.

Which one is better for travel?

SanDisk Extreme has the clearer travel spec: IP65 dust and water resistance, up to 3-meter drop protection, and an integrated loop for clipping inside a bag. Samsung T9 lists up to 3 m drop resistance under controlled conditions but publishes no IP rating, and it is the larger, heavier drive at 88 x 60 x 14 mm and 122 g.

Which one is better for mixed old and new devices?

Both handle USB-A out of the box, differently. Samsung’s data sheet lists both USB Type-C to C and Type-C to A cables for T9. SanDisk includes a USB-C to C cable and a USB-C to USB-A adapter. Either works with older machines; T9’s second cable is the tidier setup.

Should I choose by top speed alone?

No. Top speed only matters when your host port, cable, file type, and workload can use it. For many buyers, device compatibility, physical size, durability, included cable, and warranty matter more.
