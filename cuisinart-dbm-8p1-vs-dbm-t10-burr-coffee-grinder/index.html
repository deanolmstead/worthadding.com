Head-to-head burr coffee grinder comparison

Cuisinart DBM-8P1 vs DBM-T10

Both Cuisinart burr mills publish 18 grind positions, an 8-ounce bean hopper, and a grounds chamber sized for up to 32 cups. The choice comes down to interface, cup-selection range, counter fit, and availability: the DBM-8P1 uses a physical 4-to-18-cup dial and remains a current product, while the DBM-T10 adds a touchscreen, 1-to-14-cup selection, countdown timer, and manual control but is discontinued.

The short answer

Which one should most people buy?

Best burr coffee grinder pick

Cuisinart DBM-8P1

Buy the DBM-8P1 for the same published 18-position grind range and capacity in a simpler model Cuisinart still sells and supports as current.

Check DBM-8P1 on Amazon

The Cuisinart DBM-8P1 Supreme Grind Automatic Burr Mill is the better buy for most shoppers. Cuisinart publishes 18 grind positions from ultra-fine to extra-coarse, a removable 8-ounce hopper, a 4-to-18-cup slide dial, an automatic shutoff timer, and a removable chamber that can hold enough grounds for 32 cups. Its controls are ordinary switches and a dial, but that simplicity is an advantage when you want an easy-to-find current model.

The Cuisinart DBM-T10 Touchscreen Burr Grinder earns its place for interface convenience. It also publishes 18 grind positions and equivalent half-pound hopper and 32-cup chamber capacities, then adds Reveal touchscreen controls, an LCD cup/countdown display, a 1-to-14-cup selector, and manual grind control. Its listed 7.34 × 5 × 11.14-inch body is useful for measuring a coffee station.

The deciding caveat is status: Cuisinart labels the DBM-T10 discontinued. That does not make it unusable, but it means stock, seller condition, returns, and warranty coverage require more scrutiny. Unless touchscreen operation or one-cup dosing matters enough to accept that trade-off, choose the DBM-8P1.

Fit check

Choose by workflow, not matching spec counts.

The published grind range and capacities are effectively tied. Controls and model status create the useful differences.

Buy the DBM-8P1 if

  • You want a current model with uncomplicated physical controls.
  • You usually grind for four or more cups and value its 4-to-18-cup dial.
  • You want the lower-risk default when stock continuity and support matter.

Skip it if: You regularly want a one-cup setting, a visible countdown, or manual grind control.

Buy the DBM-T10 if

  • You prefer a sleeping touchscreen that wakes when you tap the power icon.
  • You want cup selection down to one cup and an LCD countdown timer.
  • You want a manual feature for customizing grind quantity.

Skip it if: You do not want to shop for a discontinued appliance or verify seller condition and warranty terms.

Same grind range

Both official pages list 18 positions from ultra-fine to extra-coarse, so neither wins on published setting count.

Different dosing range

The DBM-8P1 selects 4 to 18 cups. The DBM-T10 selects 1 to 14 cups and adds manual control.

Availability matters

The DBM-T10's discontinued status can matter more than its upgraded interface when returns and long-term ownership are priorities.

Before you buy

Check the exact listing.

  • Match the model number: DBM-8P1 versus DBM-T10 or DBM-T10P1.
  • For the discontinued DBM-T10, check whether the unit is new, used, or renewed and read return and warranty terms.
  • Measure height and lid clearance around cabinets; Cuisinart publishes 7.34 × 5 × 11.14 inches for the DBM-T10.
  • Both grinders require regular cleaning of the removable hopper, chamber, and burr area.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

We compared Cuisinart's current official product pages for selector range, hopper and grounds capacity, interface, dimensions, weight, included control features, and model status. Because both mills list the same 18-position grind range and equivalent hopper and chamber capacities, we did not invent a performance winner from tied specifications.

The recommendation instead weights buying risk and routine. The DBM-8P1 remains a current product and covers 4-to-18-cup batches with physical controls. The DBM-T10 can start at one cup and adds a countdown display and manual control, but Cuisinart now marks it discontinued. That makes the DBM-8P1 the safer general pick and the DBM-T10 a specialist interface choice.

We did not use changing prices, ratings, review counts, or unsupported claims about noise, static, retention, grind uniformity, taste, or durability. Exact stock and seller terms can change.

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Which Cuisinart burr grinder is the better pick for most people?+

The DBM-8P1 is the safer default because Cuisinart still lists it as a current product and its 4-to-18-cup slide dial is straightforward. Choose the discontinued DBM-T10 only when its touchscreen, 1-to-14-cup selector, countdown display, and manual grind control are worth seeking out.

Do the DBM-8P1 and DBM-T10 have the same grind range?+

Both have an 18-position selector spanning ultra-fine through extra-coarse. The difference is not the published setting count; it is the interface and dosing workflow.

Which grinder gives you more control over batch size?+

The DBM-T10 offers the broader published cup-selection range at 1 to 14 cups and also includes a manual feature for customizing the amount ground. The DBM-8P1 uses a 4-to-18-cup slide dial.

How much coffee do the hoppers and grind chambers hold?+

Cuisinart lists an 8-ounce removable hopper and a grind chamber sized for up to 32 cups on the DBM-8P1. It lists a half-pound removable hopper and the same up-to-32-cup chamber capacity on the DBM-T10, so their published capacities are equivalent.

Is the Cuisinart DBM-T10 discontinued?+

Yes. Cuisinart labels the DBM-T10 discontinued on its official product page. Confirm the exact seller, condition, return terms, and warranty coverage before buying remaining or marketplace stock.
