For smaller or medium hands, though, the extra size is not automatically an upgrade. A pruner that opens and closes naturally is easier to control all afternoon.

Fit check

Choose by routine, not by the classic label.

The most useful pruner is the one whose handle span and capacity fit the work you actually repeat.

Buy the FELCO 2 if

You have large hands and prefer the familiar feel of a longer, full-size bypass pruner. FELCO specifies the 2 for large right hands and publishes a 215 mm length with a 25 mm cutting diameter. Those numbers matter when mature shrubs, thicker woody stems, or a bit more reach are regular parts of your trimming routine. The tool is deliberately substantial rather than compact.

The FELCO 2 also makes sense when you want a traditional, serviceable design with forged-aluminum handles, a hardened-steel blade, a sap groove, a wire-cutting notch, and replaceable parts. It is a good fit for a home garden with enough heavier pruning to make the larger published capacity relevant, not just as an answer to the question of which model is more famous.

Skip it if: your hands are medium-sized, you do mostly light maintenance, or a 251 g, 215 mm pruner feels like more tool than you want to steer around close growth.

Buy the FELCO 6 if

You want a professional-style bypass pruner in a shorter, lighter format that FELCO identifies for medium right hands. Its 195 mm length and 210 g published weight are not promises of universal comfort, but they are meaningful reasons to start here when you spend time deadheading, tidying perennials, shaping shrubs, or making many controlled cuts in a session.

The 20 mm published cutting diameter still covers a broad set of routine garden stems. FELCO also lists forged-aluminum handles, a hardened-steel blade, a micrometric adjustment mechanism, a wire-cutting notch, and replaceable parts. In other words, the FELCO 6 reduces scale without abandoning the repairable construction and adjustment features that bring buyers to the brand.

Skip it if: you have large hands, routinely approach the larger model’s published capacity, or want the longer 215 mm form factor of the FELCO 2.

Fit-check note: published hand-size guidance and dimensions are useful filters, not a guarantee. Check the current FELCO product page and retailer listing for the exact model, orientation, included parts, and replacement-part compatibility before checkout.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

A clear recommendation should describe the trade-off, not hide it behind a generic quality claim.

These are direct competitors because both are FELCO right-hand bypass pruning shears with forged-aluminum handles, hardened-steel blades, a wire-cutting notch, an adjustment mechanism, and replaceable parts. The meaningful choice is their scale. The FELCO 2 is the classic large-hand model with a larger published 25 mm diameter, while the FELCO 6 is the shorter medium-hand alternative with a published 20 mm diameter.

We gave the default verdict to the FELCO 6 because tool fit has to be earned on repeated cuts. A 195 mm length and 210 g published weight create a practical starting point for ordinary garden maintenance, and FELCO explicitly identifies the model for medium hands. It retains the core construction and serviceability cues shoppers look for. For many gardens, a 20 mm stated maximum covers routine shrub, flower, and light woody-stem work without asking the user to manage a larger handle every time.

The FELCO 2 wins decisively when bigger hands, longer reach, or a 25 mm published cutting diameter are routine needs. Its extra 20 mm of length and 41 g of listed weight are not defects; they are the cost of a different fit. A buyer who regularly works on mature material or simply closes a large-hand handle more naturally should not downsize just because the FELCO 6 is the general recommendation.

We did not use price, ratings, review counts, popularity language, or unverified claims about how either pruner will perform on a particular plant. Published capacity is not a universal guarantee: stem condition, cutting technique, maintenance, and the exact material all affect a real cut. The result is a recommendation based on the dimensions, hand guidance, capacity, construction, and adjustment details FELCO makes available.

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Which bypass pruner should most people buy?+

The FELCO 6 is the stronger default for many home gardeners because FELCO designs it for medium hands and publishes a shorter 195 mm length, 210 g weight, and 20 mm cutting diameter. Choose the FELCO 2 when you have large hands or regularly want the larger model’s 25 mm stated cutting diameter and 215 mm length.

What is the practical difference between FELCO 2 and FELCO 6?+

The FELCO 2 is the larger classic model for large right hands, with a published 25 mm cutting diameter, 215 mm length, and 251 g weight. The FELCO 6 is a more compact right-hand model for medium hands, with a published 20 mm cutting diameter, 195 mm length, and 210 g weight. The choice is about hand fit and branch size, not a blanket quality ranking.

Are both FELCO pruners right-handed?+

Yes. FELCO lists both the FELCO 2 and FELCO 6 as right-hand pruning shears. Hand orientation and hand-size designation are part of the fit decision, so check FELCO’s current product information before ordering if you need a left-hand model or a different size.

Can both models be serviced?+

FELCO presents both models as serviceable pruning shears with replaceable parts. The company lists spare-parts collections for each model. That does not make every part interchangeable between the two models, so match any replacement blade, spring, or component to the exact model number.

Should I choose by the largest stated cutting diameter?+

Not by itself. FELCO publishes a larger 25 mm cutting diameter for the FELCO 2 and 20 mm for the FELCO 6, but a pruner still needs to sit naturally in your hand and suit the material you routinely cut. Use the published diameter as one capacity signal, then consider hand size, reach, and the condition of the stems.
