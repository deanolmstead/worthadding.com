Same rice capacity

Both 5.5-cup models list capacity as up to 5.5 cups / 1.0 liter, measured with Zojirushi's raw short-grain rice cup. Capacity is not the reason to split them.

Menu philosophy

NS-ZCC10 has the more rice-specific list, including softer or harder white rice, semi-brown, and rinse-free. NS-TSC10 trades that nuance for Steam and Cake.

Counter footprint

NS-ZCC10 is listed at 10 x 13-1/8 x 8-1/4 inches, while NS-TSC10 is 10-1/8 x 14 x 8-1/2 inches. The difference is small, but the NS-ZCC10 is the tighter fit.

Before you buy

Before you buy either Zojirushi

  • Confirm that a 5.5-cup uncooked-rice capacity is right for your household; both models also have larger 10-cup siblings.
  • Plan for counter depth, lid clearance, steam space, and a place to set the inner lid or bowl during cleanup.
  • Use the supplied rice measuring cup as the reference point; Zojirushi notes that capacity is based on an approximately 180 mL raw-rice cup.
  • If you want one appliance for rice plus sides, the NS-TSC10 makes more sense. If rice texture is the job, the NS-ZCC10 is the stronger fit.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which Zojirushi rice cooker should most rice-focused shoppers buy?

The NS-ZCC10 is the better pick for rice-focused shoppers because its official feature list emphasizes Neuro Fuzzy logic, a spherical inner cooking pan and heating system, and a broader rice-menu set.

Why would someone choose the NS-TSC10 instead?

Choose the NS-TSC10 if you will use its Steam menu, Cake menu, and included steaming basket. Those features make it more flexible even though it is less rice-specialized.

Do they have the same capacity?

Yes. The NS-ZCC10 and NS-TSC10 both list capacity up to 5.5 cups / 1.0 liter, measured with an approximately 180 mL raw short-grain rice cup.

Which one is smaller on the counter?

The NS-ZCC10 is slightly smaller by the official dimensions: 10 x 13-1/8 x 8-1/4 inches versus 10-1/8 x 14 x 8-1/2 inches for the NS-TSC10.
