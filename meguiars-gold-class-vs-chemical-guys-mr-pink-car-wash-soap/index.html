Gold Class wins the ordinary wash; Mr. Pink earns its place in a foam-first routine.

Both are 64-fluid-ounce liquid car-wash shampoos, and both manufacturers describe formulas intended to lift road grime while being gentle on protected exterior surfaces. Neither replaces vehicle or coating-maker care instructions. The useful question is whether you want a simple shampoo for a normal hand wash or a deliberately measured, foam-forward routine.

Meguiar's Gold Class is the pick for most shoppers because its named promise is clean-and-condition in one step. Meguiar's says G7164 produces rich suds, foams away dirt and contaminants, and is safe on paint types and clear coats while preserving wax protection. If dilution and foam setup are not the main event, it is the cleaner default.

Chemical Guys Mr. Pink is not a weak alternative. Chemical Guys lists 64-ounce CWS_402_64, applicable finishes from paintwork to aluminum, and a bucket direction of 1 to 3 ounces in 5 gallons. Its material emphasizes thick foam and slick lubricants, while the Amazon listing calls out foam-cannon use. Pick it when those details match your established routine; follow the current label.

Fit check

Choose by routine, not bottle color.

The better shampoo is the one whose published directions and product focus fit the tools and steps you use, rather than the one with the louder claims on a listing.

Buy Meguiar's Gold Class if

You want a 64-ounce shampoo that presents a simple wash-and-condition job: rich foam, gentle cleaning, and an explicit claim that it preserves existing wax protection on paint and clear coats. It is the more comfortable pick for a routine that begins and ends with a conventional bucket wash.

Skip it if foam-cannon use and a stated ounce-per-bucket range are key deciding factors. The official page we reviewed does not make those the product's central proposition.

Buy Chemical Guys Mr. Pink if

You want the manufacturer’s stated 1-to-3-ounce-per-5-gallon bucket direction, thick-foam positioning, and a product listing that expressly calls out foam cannons. Its published applicable-finishes list also spans paintwork, glass, plastic, rubber, stainless steel, metal, and aluminum.

Skip it if the measured, foam-forward angle is extra complexity for a basic wash. A label’s directions—not this comparison—remain the authority for use.

Quick fit check

Do not choose either bottle by a promise of a particular result on every coating or vehicle. Identify your wash tools first, use the current product directions, and treat the 64-ounce capacity as the common baseline. Gold Class is the calmer default; Mr. Pink is the more specific routine tool.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which car wash soap is better for most routine bucket washes?

Meguiar's Gold Class is the better default for most routine bucket washes. Its manufacturer positions it as a one-step wash-and-condition formula that creates rich suds and is suitable for all paint types and clear coats while preserving existing wax protection. Choose Chemical Guys Mr. Pink instead when its stated 1-to-3-ounce-per-5-gallon dilution range and very foamy, slick wash are a closer match for the way you already wash.

Can Chemical Guys Mr. Pink be used in a bucket?

Yes. Chemical Guys says to pre-rinse, shake the bottle, and add 1 to 3 ounces of Mr. Pink to a 5-gallon bucket before washing and rinsing. Follow the product label and the manufacturer's current directions for your equipment and finish.

Does Meguiar's Gold Class strip wax?

Meguiar's says Gold Class is safe on all paint types and clear coats and cleans while preserving wax protection. That describes the shampoo's intended use, not a guarantee about every coating, product layer, or wash method; follow the current label for your vehicle.

Which one is better for a foam cannon?

Chemical Guys Mr. Pink is the clearer foam-focused choice: its Amazon product listing explicitly positions the 64-ounce version for foam cannons, while Chemical Guys emphasizes thick foam and slick lubricants. Meguiar's Gold Class also makes rich suds, but the official page centers the wash-and-condition formula rather than a foam-cannon-specific routine.

Are the two bottles the same size?

Yes, the specific versions compared here are both 64-fluid-ounce bottles. Meguiar's identifies its bottle as G7164, 64 oz.; Chemical Guys lists a 64-ounce Mr. Pink option under SKU CWS_402_64. Both brands also sell other sizes, so check the listing title before ordering.
