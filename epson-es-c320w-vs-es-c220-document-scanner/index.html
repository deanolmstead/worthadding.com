ES-C320W is the better scanner for most home offices.

The ES-C320W is the more flexible purchase because Epson lists USB plus wireless scanning to computers, cloud destinations, smartphones, and tablets. That matters in small spaces: the scanner can live on a shelf, side desk, or shared work area instead of staying tethered beside one laptop.

Buyer guide

Choose by connection, not speed.

These scanners are close enough that the decision should start with where the scanner will live and how scans will move: one USB computer, several devices, mobile capture, or cloud filing.

ES-C320W

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want to scan from laptops, phones, tablets, or a shared desktop without moving a USB cable around.
  • The scanner will live on a shelf, credenza, side desk, or shared workspace where wireless placement matters.
  • You want Epson Smart Panel mobile scanning, Wi-Fi Direct, and a more flexible cloud/computer workflow.
Skip this if
  • You only scan from one computer and do not want to troubleshoot network discovery, app permissions, or Wi-Fi bands.
  • You expect wireless scans to be faster than USB; Epson's published speed class is the same.
  • You would rather put the budget toward a larger ADF or heavier-duty scanner instead of wireless convenience.
ES-C220

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • Your scanner will stay next to one Mac or Windows PC and USB is the most reliable workflow.
  • You mainly need receipts, tax records, bills, statements, forms, and small stacks digitized without a larger office scanner.
  • You want the compact Epson design without paying for wireless features you will not use.
Skip this if
  • You want to scan from a phone or tablet without treating a computer as the hub.
  • Multiple people in the household or office will share the scanner from different devices.
  • The best scanner location is not close enough for a convenient USB cable run.
Wireless changes placement

The ES-C320W's biggest advantage is not raw speed. It is the freedom to place the scanner where paper enters the workflow, then send scans to a computer, mobile device, or cloud path without keeping it beside one laptop.

20 sheets is a small-stack limit

A 20-page ADF is right for receipts, bills, statements, school forms, and small record batches. If you routinely scan folders at a time, a larger desktop scanner with a bigger feeder will be less tedious.

Flexible path needs setup

Epson's flexible scan path is useful for varied originals, but passports, folded originals, and fragile or unusual sheets can require carrier sheets and settings changes. Do not treat either model like a careless pile feeder.

Before you buy

Check your desk, paper pile, and software path first.

  • Measure the scanner location with input and output handling in mind, not just the body footprint.
  • Decide whether scans must start from a phone or tablet. If yes, ES-C320W is the more natural fit.
  • For long paper, passports, folded originals, and delicate documents, read Epson's loading guidance before feeding valuable originals.
  • Download expectations matter: Epson notes that ScanSmart and mobile workflows can require app downloads, internet access, and supported devices.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which Epson compact document scanner is better for most people?

ES-C320W is the better default if you want the same compact scanning format plus Wi-Fi, Wi-Fi Direct, mobile scanning, and easier shared placement. ES-C220 is the simpler USB-only pick if one computer will always handle scans.

Do ES-C320W and ES-C220 scan at the same speed?

Epson lists both models at up to 30 ppm and 60 ipm for letter-size scans at 300 dpi, so the main buying split is connection and workflow rather than speed.

Which one is better for phones and tablets?

ES-C320W is the better fit for phones and tablets because Epson lists wireless scanning to mobile devices with the Epson Smart Panel app. ES-C220 is a USB Type-C scanner for a connected computer workflow.

Can either scanner handle receipts and passports?

Both are compact sheet-fed document scanners with a flexible scan path and optional carrier-sheet support for certain document types. Receipts, cards, folded originals, and passports require the right scan path, settings, and carrier sheet where Epson calls for one.

Should I step up just for wireless?

Step up if the scanner will live away from your computer, serve more than one device, or feed a mobile/cloud workflow. Stay with ES-C220 if the scanner will sit beside one computer and USB is easier to trust.
