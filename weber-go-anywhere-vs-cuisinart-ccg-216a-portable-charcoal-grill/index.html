Head-to-head portable charcoal grill comparison

Weber Go-Anywhere vs Cuisinart CCG-216A

Weber’s Go-Anywhere is a rectangular charcoal grill with 160 square inches of cooking area, a two-piece grate, a Char-Rail insert, and a listed 11.5-pound weight. Cuisinart’s 16-inch CCG-216A lists 256 square inches of cooking area, a locking lid, a dual vent system, and a 5.85-pound weight. The better choice is about charcoal control versus cooking room and carry weight, not a vague promise about better food.

The short answer

Which one should most people buy?

The better default is the grill that gives a car-camper more control over charcoal placement without becoming hard to pack.

Weber Go-Anywhere

The Weber Go-Anywhere is the more capable all-around pick for a buyer who drives to the campsite, tailgate, park, or patio. Weber lists a 160-square-inch cooking area, a two-piece cooking grate for adding charcoal, and a Char-Rail insert that lets the owner arrange coals for direct heat or move them to one side for indirect cooking. Those are concrete format advantages when a small charcoal grill needs to do more than one simple job.

The Cuisinart CCG-216A has a genuine counterargument. Its official page lists 256 square inches of cooking space — 96 more than the Weber — and only 5.85 pounds, compared with Weber’s listed 11.5 pounds. It also has a dual vent system, a locking lid, a four-legged design, and an ash catcher. Choose it when the larger grate and lighter package are the priorities and you want a round 16-inch grill with the bigger published grate-area number.

For most people, though, the Weber’s rectangular layout and charcoal-management hardware are the more useful distinction. Its legs fold up to lock the lid in place; its two lid dampers and bowl vents handle airflow; and the two-piece grate avoids removing the whole grate just to add fuel. This is not a claim that one grill will cook every meal better. It is a recommendation for the buyer who wants a portable grill with a more flexible charcoal setup, while acknowledging that the Cuisinart wins on grate area and carry weight.

Fit check

Choose by routine, not grill snobbery.

Both are portable charcoal options. Start with the drive, the carry, and how you expect to manage coals after the first round of food.

Weber Go-Anywhere

Buy the Weber Go-Anywhere if

Your portable grill normally stays in the vehicle until you reach the campsite, tailgate, picnic table, or backyard. Weber’s 11.5-pound listed weight is manageable for that kind of car-based trip, and the rectangular body uses space differently from a small round kettle. The two-piece grate lets you add charcoal as needed, while the Char-Rail insert is the stated way to arrange coals for high direct heat or put them to one side for indirect cooking.

Skip it if: You expect to carry the grill any real distance or want the largest grate in this class. At 11.5 pounds, it is the heavier option beside Cuisinart’s listed 5.85 pounds, and its 160 square inches trail the CCG-216A’s 256.

Cuisinart CCG-216A

Buy the Cuisinart CCG-216A if

You drive to the campsite, tailgate, beach, or patio and want the portable grill to handle a more flexible small-group meal. The 256-square-inch grate is the reason to step up. Cuisinart also specifies a locking lid, dual vent system, four-legged design, and ash catcher, so the added surface does not mean abandoning the category’s basic portability.

Skip it if: You specifically want Weber’s two-piece grate access and Char-Rail insert for moving coals into an indirect zone. The CCG-216A’s official page describes its vent arrangement, but it does not list those Weber-specific charcoal-management pieces.

Quick fit check: Pick the Cuisinart if the grill is cargo you need to carry and feed a small group from. Pick the Weber if it will mostly ride in the car and you want more control over where the coals sit once you are cooking.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

This decision is not settled by a broad claim that one charcoal grill is “better.” The published figures point in two directions. Cuisinart lists 256 square inches of cooking area, which is 96 square inches more than Weber’s listed 160 square inches. Cuisinart also lists the CCG-216A at 5.85 pounds; Weber lists the Go-Anywhere at 11.5 pounds. Those are meaningful advantages for a buyer who wants the roomier compact unit with the lighter stated carry.

We weighted the Weber’s charcoal-management format more heavily for the general recommendation. Weber explicitly describes a two-piece cooking grate for adding charcoal and a Char-Rail insert for arranging coals for direct heat or indirect cooking. It also lists two lid dampers and bowl vents. Those features are not a performance test, but they are published hardware differences that change how a buyer can set up the grill during a cook.

Then we considered transport as a complete routine. Weber says the legs fold up and tuck to lock the lid in place, so the Go-Anywhere remains designed to travel. Yet its listed 11.5-pound weight is nearly twice the Cuisinart’s listed 5.85 pounds. The CCG-216A’s locking lid, dual vent system, and ash catcher make it the more persuasive pick whenever grate room and a lighter lift matter more than charcoal placement hardware.

We did not infer cook times, temperature precision, smoke output, durability beyond the stated materials, or a serving count from these specifications. Fuel, weather, food, setup, and use vary. The recommendation is deliberately narrower: for a portable grill that will mostly be transported by vehicle and used across different charcoal layouts, the Weber’s format earns the default. For the roomier grate at roughly half the carry weight, the Cuisinart’s published figures are the better reason to choose differently.

Before checkout

Questions before checkout.

Which portable charcoal grill is the better pick for most people?

The Weber Go-Anywhere is the better pick for most people choosing between these two portable charcoal grills. Weber lists 160 square inches of cooking area, an 11.5-pound weight, two-piece grate access for adding charcoal, a Char-Rail insert, two lid dampers, and bowl vents. Choose the Cuisinart CCG-216A when its 256-square-inch grate and 5.85-pound carry matter more than the Weber’s charcoal-management hardware.

How much more cooking area does the Cuisinart CCG-216A have?

Cuisinart lists 256 square inches of steel cooking grate for the CCG-216A. Weber lists 160 square inches of primary cooking area for the Go-Anywhere, so the Cuisinart has 96 more square inches on the published product pages. Cooking-area figures show available grate space; they do not promise a particular serving count or cooking result.

Which grill is easier to carry?

Cuisinart lists the CCG-216A at 5.85 pounds, while Weber lists the Go-Anywhere at 11.5 pounds. The CCG-216A also has a locking lid. The Weber’s legs fold up to lock its lid in place, but its higher listed weight makes the Cuisinart the lighter-carry choice.

Do both grills provide airflow control?

Yes. Weber describes two lid dampers and bowl vents for airflow on the Go-Anywhere. Cuisinart describes the CCG-216A as using a lower air-inlet vent on the fire bowl and an air-exhaust vent on the lid. Those manufacturer descriptions establish adjustable airflow features, not a guarantee of a specific temperature, cook time, or food outcome.

What is the practical difference between the grill formats?

The Weber Go-Anywhere uses a rectangular cooking format with a two-piece grate and Char-Rail insert for arranging coals. The Cuisinart CCG-216A is a 16-inch round portable grill with a steel grate, locking lid, dual vent arrangement, and ash catcher. Pick by how you plan to pack, fuel, and use the grill rather than by an assumed performance ranking.
