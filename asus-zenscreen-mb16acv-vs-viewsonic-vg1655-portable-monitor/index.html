Verdict

Which portable monitor should most people buy?

Choose ViewSonic VG1655 if you want the more forgiving travel desk: USB-C on either side of the power equation, mini HDMI for more devices, built-in speakers, and a wide built-in stand. Choose ASUS ZenScreen MB16ACV if your priority is a thin single-port ASUS screen with a tripod socket and DisplayLink driver support for USB Type-A hosts.

Best overall

ViewSonic VG1655

It wins because the extra ports change real setup options: two USB-C ports with 60W two-way power, mini HDMI, a headphone jack, speakers, included cables, and a stand that tilts from flat to 60 degrees.

VG1655 is the safer buy for mixed-device portable work.

The panels look similar on paper: both are 15.6-inch Full HD IPS displays with 250 cd/m2 typical brightness, anti-glare surfaces, 800:1 contrast, and 60Hz refresh. The difference is everything around the panel. ViewSonic gives you two USB-C ports, 60W two-way power behavior, mini HDMI, dual 0.8W speakers, and a broader stand angle.

ASUS still has a real lane. ZenScreen MB16ACV is thinner, has a 1/4-inch tripod socket, and ASUS calls out hybrid USB-C support with a driver path for USB Type-A. That can matter for older work laptops. The tradeoff is that you give up HDMI, pass-through power, speakers, and the second USB-C port.

Buyer guide

Start with ports, not screen size.

Both monitors land in the same portable 1080p screen class. The right choice depends on whether your second screen must work with USB-C only, HDMI devices, low-power laptops, older USB-A ports, or a fixed stand setup.

ViewSonic VG1655

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want one portable screen for USB-C laptops, mini HDMI sources, tablets, and occasional media use.
  • Pass-through power matters because you want an adapter or power bank to help feed the monitor-and-laptop setup.
  • You prefer a built-in stand with a wide 0-to-60-degree range and both portrait and landscape placement.
Skip this if
  • Your bag setup is strictly one USB-C laptop and you do not need HDMI, speakers, or power pass-through.
  • You want the thinnest body in this pair; ASUS lists a slimmer depth.
  • You planned to mount the monitor on a tripod or arm without adding a separate accessory.
ASUS ZenScreen MB16ACV

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want a slim portable display for a compatible USB-C laptop and do not need HDMI fallback.
  • Your older laptop may need USB Type-A support and you are comfortable using the ASUS driver path.
  • You want the 1/4-inch tripod socket for a cleaner raised desk or temporary workstation setup.
Skip this if
  • You need mini HDMI for cameras, consoles, docked devices, or laptops without USB-C video output.
  • You want USB-C pass-through power, a second USB-C port, built-in speakers, or a headphone jack.
  • You are buying for a shared office where driver installs and USB-C capability vary by computer.
Dual USB-C changes power

VG1655 can sit between a power source and a laptop in more setups. That does not guarantee every laptop will charge perfectly, but it gives you more options than a single USB-C display input.

HDMI is the fallback path

Mini HDMI gives ViewSonic a practical escape route for devices that do not send video over USB-C. HDMI still needs separate power, so keep the adapter and cable plan in the bag.

USB-A needs expectations

ASUS has the more interesting older-laptop path because it advertises USB Type-A driver support. Treat that as a managed setup, not a universal plug-and-play guarantee.

Before you buy

Check your laptop port, power plan, and viewing setup.

  • Confirm your USB-C port supports DisplayPort Alt Mode if you expect one-cable video.
  • If your source uses HDMI, budget space for the mini HDMI cable plus a USB-C power source.
  • Think about stand height. A built-in portable stand is convenient, but neither monitor raises the panel to eye level by itself.
  • For locked-down work laptops, check whether DisplayLink or vendor utilities can be installed before choosing the USB-A-friendly ASUS path.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which portable monitor is better for most people?

ViewSonic VG1655 is the better default because it keeps the same 15.6-inch Full HD class while adding dual USB-C ports with 60W two-way power, mini HDMI, built-in speakers, and a wider stand range.

Why would you buy the ASUS ZenScreen MB16ACV instead?

Buy the ASUS if you want the slimmer ASUS design, a fold-out kickstand, a tripod socket, antibacterial treatment, and a driver-supported USB Type-A path for an older laptop.

Do both monitors use a 15.6-inch 1080p IPS panel?

Yes. ASUS and ViewSonic list 15.6-inch Full HD IPS panels with 1920 x 1080 resolution, 250 cd/m2 typical brightness, 800:1 contrast, anti-glare surfaces, and 60Hz refresh rates.

Does the ViewSonic VG1655 work over HDMI?

ViewSonic lists one mini HDMI input and includes a mini HDMI cable. HDMI sends video only, so plan on USB-C or adapter power for the monitor.

What compatibility should I check before buying?

Check whether your laptop USB-C port supports DisplayPort Alt Mode and enough power for one-cable use. If it does not, the ViewSonic mini HDMI path or the ASUS DisplayLink driver path may be more important than the panel specs.
