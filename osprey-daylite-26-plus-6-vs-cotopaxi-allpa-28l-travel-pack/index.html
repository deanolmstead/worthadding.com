Buyer guide

Choose by airline fit and packing style.

These bags overlap on short trips, but they solve different problems: the Osprey protects flexibility and low weight, while the Cotopaxi protects organization and laptop-carry structure.

Osprey Daylite 26+6

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want a bag that starts at a slim 6-inch depth before using the expansion zipper.
  • You pack light on departure but often need extra room for a jacket, souvenirs, or laundry on the return trip.
  • You care more about low carry weight and water-bottle pockets than a heavily divided internal layout.
Skip this if
  • You want a published 15-inch padded laptop sleeve and a dedicated tablet-style office layout.
  • You prefer a waist belt or lockable main zipper hardware for a more travel-pack-like setup.
  • You dislike managing an expansion zipper and want the full usable volume available all the time.
Cotopaxi Allpa 28L

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want a suitcase-like clamshell interior with separate mesh pockets for organized packing.
  • You carry a 15-inch laptop and want the sleeve called out clearly in the product specs.
  • You prefer a more structured bag with a tuckable waist belt, lockable zippers, and multiple grab handles.
Skip this if
  • You are trying to stay as close as possible to under-seat personal-item dimensions on budget airlines.
  • You want the lighter bag; the Cotopaxi is about 11 ounces heavier by published weight.
  • You mostly need a commute-friendly travel daypack rather than a mini suitcase on your back.
Depth changes airline fit

The Osprey starts at 6 inches deep, while the Cotopaxi is listed at 9 inches deep. That difference matters most when a gate agent or under-seat bin is strict.

Expansion is useful but conditional

The Osprey adds 6 liters when expanded, which is excellent for overflow packing. Use that extra space with care if the bag still needs to pass as a personal item.

Organization adds weight

The Cotopaxi gives you more built-in structure, lockable zippers, and a waist belt, but its published 2 lb 8 oz weight is noticeably higher than the Osprey.

Before you buy

Before you buy a compact travel backpack

  • Check the exact personal-item dimensions for the airlines you fly most; neither brand can guarantee every route or aircraft bin.
  • Measure your laptop, not just the screen class. Protective cases and thicker work laptops can change sleeve fit.
  • Think about walking time. A structured travel pack helps organization, but low weight matters if you carry it across terminals or city transit.
  • Pack soft goods near the front panel when using clamshell bags so hard objects do not press into the laptop side.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which bag is better for personal-item flights?

The Osprey is the safer pick for personal-item-focused travelers because its listed dimensions are slimmer before expansion. Always check your airline's current size limit before flying.

Which bag is better for laptop carry?

The Cotopaxi is the clearer laptop pick because Cotopaxi publishes a 15-inch padded fleece-lined laptop sleeve. The Osprey has a simple laptop sleeve, but its specs are less laptop-first.

Can the Osprey replace the Cotopaxi for a weekend trip?

Yes, if you pack light or use the 6-liter expansion. The Cotopaxi is better if you want the organization built into the pack instead of relying on packing cubes.

Which one is easier to carry all day?

The Osprey has the advantage on published weight. The Cotopaxi adds a tuckable waist belt and more structure, which can help some loads but also makes the pack heavier.

Should I choose by volume alone?

No. The difference between 26+6 liters and 28 liters matters less than depth, laptop fit, organization, and whether you need the bag to work under an airline seat.
