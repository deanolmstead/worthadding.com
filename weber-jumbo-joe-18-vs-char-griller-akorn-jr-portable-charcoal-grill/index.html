Head-to-head portable charcoal grill comparison

Weber Jumbo Joe 18 vs Char-Griller AKORN Jr.

The Weber Jumbo Joe is a roomy 18-inch portable kettle with 240 square inches of listed cooking area, a plated-steel grate, and a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock. The Char-Griller AKORN Jr. is a more compact, insulated kamado-style grill with 155 square inches, a cast-iron grate, dual dampers, and a stated 200–700°F range. Weber wins on practical grate space and simple transport; Char-Griller wins when the cook is built around controlled heat.

These are genuinely comparable because both are compact charcoal grills meant to leave a large permanent grill behind. Both have a covered fire, a lid, a grate, a dedicated ash-management feature, and carry-minded design. But they ask for different routines. The Weber is an 18-inch portable kettle: open it, arrange charcoal, grill, use the lid and dampers as needed, empty the catcher once cool, and lock the lid for the trip home. The AKORN Jr. is a compact kamado-style unit, built around insulation and adjustable airflow rather than an open kettle format.

For most shoppers, the Weber is the more useful default. Weber publishes 240 square inches of primary and total cooking area, compared with Char-Griller’s listed 155 square inches. That difference matters when a small patio cookout turns from a few burgers into a mixed grate of vegetables, chicken, and buns. Weber also lists a 19.7-inch-high by 19.7-inch-wide by 20.5-inch-deep lid-closed footprint, an 18-inch diameter, plated-steel grate, two rust-resistant aluminum dampers, ash catcher, and a lid lock that doubles as a lid holder. It is specific, legible, and suited to the common task: make charcoal grilling portable without adding a second hobby.

The AKORN Jr. is the right alternative when the reason to shop is not extra grate area but thermal behavior. Char-Griller lists a 200–700°F range, triple-walled insulation, dual adjustable dampers, a cast-iron grate, an EasyDump ash pan, and 155 square inches of cooking space. The maker frames it for both high-heat searing and lower-temperature smoking. That is a meaningful trade-off, not a minor feature advantage. Buy the Weber unless you will actually use the AKORN Jr.’s insulated, damper-managed format often enough to make its smaller cooking area and 35.7-pound published weight worthwhile.

Fit check

Choose by cook pattern, not grill mythology.

A portable charcoal grill earns its place through the recurring sequence: load it, light it, arrange the food, manage heat, deal with ash after it cools, and put it away.

Buy Weber Jumbo Joe if

You want the portable grill to behave like a scaled-down version of a familiar backyard kettle. The published 240-square-inch grate is the major practical advantage. It gives you more room for a group meal without turning the unit into a full-size cart grill, and it is a tangible number rather than a vague claim about feeding a crowd. Weber also gives a real lid-closed footprint, so you can compare it with a car trunk, balcony shelf, or garage corner before it arrives.

The carry setup is equally important. Weber says the Tuck-N-Carry lid lock secures the lid to the bowl and doubles as a lid holder while grilling. Along with the ash catcher, plated-steel grate, and two dampers, that points to a simple routine for picnics, tailgates, beach cookouts, and compact patios. It does not promise that any one person will find the grill easy to carry; it does make the Weber the more direct choice for the shopper trying to minimize moving parts.

Quick reality check: the Jumbo Joe is still a charcoal grill. Plan for a stable, appropriate outdoor surface, adequate clearance, and a cool-down period before packing it away. The recommendation is about product format and maker-published specs, not a substitute for the manufacturer’s safety and use instructions.

Buy Char-Griller AKORN Jr. if

You want compact charcoal cooking to include deliberate low-and-slow sessions as well as direct grilling. Char-Griller publishes a 200–700°F range, triple-walled insulation, and dual adjustable dampers. Those are the core reasons to select this model. They are more useful to a cook who wants to manage airflow and heat over time than to someone whose typical outing is a quick grill-and-go meal.

The AKORN Jr. also gives you a cast-iron cooking grate, EasyDump ash pan, side handles, and 155 square inches of stated space. Its compact width and depth can make storage easier, while its published 35.7-pound weight is a reason to pause before treating it as an effortless one-handed travel grill. Pick it when that weight and smaller grate are acceptable exchanges for the insulated kamado format. Do not choose it merely because it sounds more specialized.

Char-Griller’s feature list supports high-heat searing and lower-temperature smoking, but a result still depends on fuel, weather, setup, and the cook’s own method. If you will not use that adjustable-damper routine, the Weber’s simpler larger-grate layout is the more sensible purchase.

Quick fit check: measure the storage spot, think about who will carry the grill, count the food that usually needs grate space at once, and decide honestly whether you want quick direct grilling or will repeatedly manage a lower-temperature charcoal cook. Those answers should outweigh visual style.

The criteria behind the pick.

We treated this as a portable charcoal-cooking decision. Listed grate area, external footprint, construction, airflow controls, ash handling, carry arrangement, and the actual cooking routine mattered more than prestige or price.

Routine checked

We considered the moments that determine whether a portable grill gets used: lifting and storing it, getting charcoal arranged, fitting a typical meal on the grate, managing airflow, using the lid, cleaning up cooled ash, and choosing whether the cook calls for quick direct heat or sustained controlled heat. A good recommendation should make that recurring pattern easier without claiming a universal cooking result.

Weber gives most shoppers a better all-around portable charcoal package: 240 listed square inches, a compact published lid-closed footprint, plated-steel grate, ash catcher, two dampers, and a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock. The AKORN Jr. is a real alternative rather than a lesser version. Its 200–700°F stated range, insulation, cast iron, and adjustable dampers are stronger reasons for a buyer who specifically wants a small kamado-style grill.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which portable charcoal grill is the better pick for most people?

The Weber Jumbo Joe 18 is the better pick for most people who want a compact, straightforward charcoal grill for a patio, picnic, or tailgate. Weber lists 240 square inches of primary cooking area in a 19.7 by 19.7 by 20.5-inch lid-closed footprint, plus a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock and plated-steel grate. Choose the Char-Griller AKORN Jr. when its insulated kamado body, 200–700°F stated range, cast-iron grate, and smoking-oriented airflow control are the reason you are shopping.

How much cooking space does each grill have?

Weber lists 240 square inches of primary and total cooking area for the 18-inch Jumbo Joe. Char-Griller lists 155 square inches of total cooking space for the AKORN Jr. The Weber therefore gives more listed grate area, while the AKORN Jr. is the smaller, insulated kamado-format option.

Can the Char-Griller AKORN Jr. handle low-and-slow cooking?

Char-Griller states a 200–700°F temperature range for the AKORN Jr. and describes dual adjustable dampers, triple-walled insulation, and both low-temperature smoking and high-heat searing uses. Those published features make it the more purpose-built choice for a shopper who specifically wants that temperature-control format. Use the maker’s current instructions and suitable fuel setup for any cook.

What makes the Weber Jumbo Joe easier to take along?

Weber describes the 18-inch Jumbo Joe as compact and lightweight and includes a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock that secures the lid to the bowl and doubles as a lid holder. Its 18-inch kettle format and 19.7 by 19.7 by 20.5-inch lid-closed dimensions are a simpler fit for a shopper prioritizing a straightforward carry-and-grill routine. Confirm the packed size and weight against your own vehicle and carrying setup.

Do both grills have ash-management features?

Yes. Weber lists an ash catcher for the Jumbo Joe. Char-Griller lists an EasyDump ash pan for the AKORN Jr. The practical difference is not that one has no ash solution; it is whether you prefer the simpler kettle arrangement or the AKORN Jr.’s insulated kamado construction and adjustable-damper workflow.
