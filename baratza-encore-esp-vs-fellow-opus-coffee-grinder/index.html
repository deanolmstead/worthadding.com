Head-to-head coffee grinder comparison

Baratza Encore ESP vs Fellow Opus

The Baratza Encore ESP and Fellow Opus both aim at the same home bar: one grinder for espresso, pour-over, drip, French press, and cold brew. Baratza is the better pick if espresso dialing and serviceable long-term ownership matter most. Fellow is the cleaner, shorter all-purpose grinder for single-dose routines and low-mess counters.

Buy the Baratza Encore ESP if espresso is even part of the plan.

The Encore ESP is the safer recommendation because Baratza built the adjustment map around two jobs: micro-steps from 1 through 20 for espresso and macro-steps from 21 through 40 for filter brewing. The Fellow Opus is still a very good alternative. It has 41+ settings, 40 mm conical burrs, a 110 g capacity, anti-static technology, low retention, and a shorter body that fits more easily under cabinets. Pick it if you want a compact, design-forward, single-dose grinder and your espresso expectations are occasional rather than obsessive.

Buyer guide

Choose by the coffee you actually make.

Both grinders are meant to cover more than one brew style. The real split is whether you want Baratza's clearer espresso/filter setting map or Fellow's cleaner single-dose workflow and shorter counter profile.

Baratza Encore ESP

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You own, or expect to buy, a home espresso machine and want a grinder with a finer adjustment zone for dialing shots.
  • You also brew drip, pour-over, AeroPress, French press, or cold brew and want one grinder with a published filter range.
  • You like long-term ownership: Baratza publishes manuals, parts diagrams, and support documents for its grinders.
Skip this if
  • You want the shortest grinder possible under low cabinets; the Encore ESP is taller than the Opus on paper.
  • You single-dose every morning and want a catch cup and grind path designed primarily around low retention and less mess.
  • You care more about quiet visual design and countertop aesthetics than a repair-friendly appliance feel.
Fellow Opus

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You weigh each dose and want a grinder with a spouted catch cup, anti-static technology, and low-retention design.
  • You move between pour-over, drip, French press, AeroPress, cold brew, and occasional espresso without wanting a tall tower grinder.
  • You want a shorter appliance that looks clean on an open coffee bar and stores more easily under cabinets.
Skip this if
  • Espresso dialing is the main reason you are upgrading; Baratza's published espresso-first adjustment zone is easier to explain and follow.
  • You want the most obvious replacement-parts and service path from the grinder brand.
  • You dislike inner-ring or calibration tweaks and want a simpler numbered adjustment map.
Adjustment range

Both grinders publish espresso-to-cold-brew coverage, but Baratza's split range is easier for a beginner to interpret: lower numbers for espresso, higher numbers for filter coffee.

Retention and mess

Fellow's low-retention path, anti-static system, and spouted catch cup matter if you weigh beans per drink. Less leftover coffee means fewer stale grounds carrying into the next brew.

Counter height

The Encore ESP is narrower but taller. The Opus is shorter, so it is easier to tuck under cabinets, though you should still leave room to load beans and remove the catch cup.

Before you buy

Check these grinder-specific fit points first.

  • Do not treat either grinder as a commercial espresso setup. They are home all-purpose grinders with espresso capability, not cafe equipment.
  • Measure under-cabinet clearance with the hopper or load bin open, not just the listed height.
  • Decide whether you keep beans in a hopper or weigh a single dose. Hopper storage favors a simple repeatable setup; single dosing favors the Opus workflow.
  • Plan for cleaning. Burr grinders hold coffee oils and fines, and anti-static parts still need brushing to keep working well.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which grinder is better for espresso?

The Baratza Encore ESP is the better pick if espresso is part of the plan because its first 20 grind steps are tuned for finer espresso adjustment and it includes a dosing cup workflow.

Which grinder is cleaner for single dosing?

The Fellow Opus is the better fit if mess control and single-dose handling matter most. Fellow lists anti-static technology, low grind retention, a spouted catch cup, and a 110 g grind capacity.

Can either grinder handle pour-over and drip coffee?

Yes. Both grinders are positioned for brewed coffee. Baratza splits its 40 steps between espresso and filter ranges, while Fellow lists 41+ settings for espresso, pour-over, electric coffee makers, French press, AeroPress, and cold brew.

Which grinder takes less counter space?

The Fellow Opus is shorter and lower on paper at 210 mm x 129 mm x 268 mm. The Baratza Encore ESP is taller at 14.5 cm x 14.5 cm x 33.5 cm, so it needs more under-cabinet height.

Which one should a beginner buy?

Buy the Baratza Encore ESP if you want the clearest path into espresso and brewed coffee tuning. Buy the Fellow Opus if you mostly want a compact all-purpose grinder with cleaner dosing and less retention fuss.
