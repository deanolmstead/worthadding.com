The Weber Smokey Joe is the more complete default for a buyer who wants a compact kettle for the patio, park, tailgate, or campsite. Weber specifies a porcelain-enamel bowl and lid, a plated-steel cooking grate, a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock that also works as a lid holder, an ash catcher, and both lid and bowl dampers. It also states a 10-year limited warranty for the bowl and lid against rust-through and burn-through. Those are tangible ownership details, not claims about an untested cooking result.

The Cuisinart CCG-190 has a real advantage for the person who prioritizes stated capacity and a low published carry weight. Its official page lists 196 square inches of cooking space—49 more than Weber’s listed 147—and a 4.2-pound weight. Cuisinart also lists a chrome-plated grate, dual adjustable vents, three secure lid locks, an enamel-coated firebox, and a mess-free ash base. Choose it when that larger published grate figure and a clearly stated lightweight carry spec are more useful than the Weber kettle’s materials and warranty.

For most buyers, the Weber’s simple, durable kettle configuration is the stronger reason to choose. Its ash catcher, locking lid, lid-holder function, and published bowl-and-lid warranty describe a practical routine from setup through cleanup. It is not a claim that every meal will cook better. It is a narrower call: for a small charcoal grill that will be packed in a vehicle and used repeatedly, the Smokey Joe offers the better all-around ownership case.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

This is not a broad contest about which charcoal grill makes better food. The manufacturer pages describe different purchase cases. Cuisinart lists 196 square inches of cooking space, while Weber lists 147 square inches. The 49-square-inch difference is meaningful for a shopper trying to maximize stated grate area in a small format. Cuisinart also lists the CCG-190 at 4.2 pounds, a useful hard number when a buyer expects to carry the grill from car to site.

We weighted the Weber’s ownership hardware more heavily for the general recommendation. Its product page names the Tuck-N-Carry lid lock, which also serves as a lid holder, plus an ash catcher, a porcelain-enamel bowl and lid, a plated-steel cooking grate, and two rust-resistant aluminum dampers. Weber also publishes a 10-year limited warranty for the bowl and lid. These details identify a familiar kettle workflow without requiring a claim about heat performance that the sources do not establish.

The Cuisinart remains the sharper pick when its numbers and compact features map directly to the routine. Its official description names a chrome-plated grate, dual vents, three secure lid locks, enamel-coated firebox, and ash base. That is enough to make the case for a buyer who wants more stated grate area, knows a low listed carry weight matters, and does not need the Weber’s particular lid-holder or warranty package.

We did not infer cook times, temperature precision, smoke output, durability beyond stated materials and warranty, or a serving count from these specifications. Fuel, weather, food, setup, and use vary. The recommendation is deliberately practical: choose Weber for the compact kettle package; choose Cuisinart when published area and carry weight take priority.

Before choosing, measure the spot where the grill will ride and the surface where it will sit. Weber publishes its closed dimensions, while this comparison intentionally does not substitute a retailer dimension for Cuisinart when the manufacturer page gives a different set of decision details. Check the exact finish, included parts, and current manufacturer instructions for the listing you intend to buy. The point is not to turn a compact kettle into a full-size grill; it is to choose the small, portable format whose stated hardware best fits the routine you actually have.

Which kettle charcoal grill is the better pick for most people?+

The Weber Smokey Joe is the better pick for most people choosing between these compact kettle charcoal grills. Weber lists a 147-square-inch cooking area, a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock that can also hold the lid, an ash catcher, and lid and bowl dampers. Choose the Cuisinart CCG-190 when its listed 196 square inches of cooking area and 4.2-pound published weight are more important than the Weber’s porcelain-enamel kettle construction and stated 10-year bowl-and-lid warranty.

Which grill has more published cooking area?+

Cuisinart lists 196 square inches of cooking space for the CCG-190. Weber lists 147 square inches of primary and total cooking area for the Smokey Joe. That is a 49-square-inch difference in the manufacturers’ published figures; grate area is not a guarantee of a particular serving count or cooking result.

Which grill is easier to carry?+

Cuisinart lists the CCG-190 at 4.2 pounds and describes three secure lid locks. Weber describes the Smokey Joe as compact and lightweight and gives it a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock, but the official product page used here does not list a product weight. If a published weight is the deciding criterion, Cuisinart supplies the clearer figure.

Do both grills provide airflow control?+

Yes. Weber lists two rust-resistant aluminum dampers and describes lid and bowl dampers for heat control on the Smokey Joe. Cuisinart describes a dual venting system with adjustable vents on the CCG-190. These are published control features, not a promise of a specific temperature or food outcome.

What is the practical difference between these two kettle grills?+

The Smokey Joe is a porcelain-enamel kettle with a Tuck-N-Carry lid lock, ash catcher, plated-steel grate, and a stated 10-year limited bowl-and-lid warranty. The CCG-190 is a compact 14-inch grill with a larger listed grate area, chrome-plated grate, enamel-coated firebox, ash base, dual vents, and three lid locks. Pick by cooking-space needs, carry routine, cleanup features, and the exact model details rather than an assumed performance ranking.
