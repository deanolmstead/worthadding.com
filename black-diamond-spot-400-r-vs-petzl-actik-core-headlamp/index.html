Head-to-head rechargeable headlamp comparison

Black Diamond Spot 400-R vs Petzl ACTIK CORE

These are compact rechargeable headlamps for the same after-dark jobs—trail starts, camp chores, running, and a backup light in the car—but they take different approaches to power. Black Diamond’s Spot 400-R is the lighter, integrated-battery choice with a 400-lumen high mode and IP67 rating. Petzl’s ACTIK CORE brings more peak output, a wide-or-mixed beam, and a rechargeable battery that can be swapped for three AAAs when charging is not the plan.

Verdict

Which one should most people buy?

The Petzl ACTIK CORE is the more flexible choice for most people because it pairs higher published output with a mixed beam and a true backup-power option.

Best rechargeable headlamp pickPetzl ACTIK CORE, E065AB

Buy Petzl for hiking, running, or multi-night trips where 625 lumens, a 115 m max distance, and AAA fallback matter more than maximum water resistance.

The ACTIK CORE wins the broader recommendation because Petzl builds more routes out of a dead or mismatched charging routine. Its included 1250 mAh CORE rechargeable battery supports a published 625-lumen MAX POWER mode, and Petzl says the HYBRID CONCEPT design also accepts three AAA/LR03 batteries. That is a practical distinction for a traveler, backpacker, or person who keeps batteries in a trail kit. Petzl also specifies a wide or mixed beam, three white-light levels, red light, a tilt plate, charge indicator, reflective washable headband, and a storage pouch that can become a lantern.

The Spot 400-R is still the better pick for a simpler, more weather-focused compact light. Black Diamond lists 400 lumens, 86 g weight, an integrated 1500 mAh Li-ion battery, PowerTap instant high-output adjustment, digital lockout, red night vision, and IP67 protection. Its high-mode 100 m distance is close to Petzl’s 115 m claim, and its waterproof rating is materially stronger than Petzl’s IPX4 weather-resistance rating. Choose it when you charge routinely and want a straightforward headlamp for wet camp tasks, pre-dawn walks, and a compact everyday kit.

Fit check

Choose by power plan and conditions.

At 86 g versus 88 g, weight is effectively a tie. The decision is whether stronger weather protection or broader battery flexibility is more useful to your actual after-dark routine.

Buy Black Diamond if

You want one sealed-in rechargeable system.

Pick the Spot 400-R when your headlamp normally gets charged at home, in the car, or from a power bank and you value a compact unit without a separate battery module to manage. Black Diamond lists an integrated 1500 mAh Li-ion battery, a battery meter, brightness memory, PowerTap, digital lockout, proximity and distance modes, and red-night-vision dimming and strobe. Its published IP67 rating is the notable differentiator for use around sustained rain or wet gear.

Skip it if AAA replacement power is a requirement. The official specification lists the integrated Li-ion battery, not a user-selectable AAA mode. Its 400-lumen ceiling is still substantial, but it gives up Petzl’s 625-lumen top setting and mixed-beam description.

Buy Petzl if

Your plans do not always include a charging outlet.

Pick the ACTIK CORE when you want the included rechargeable battery for ordinary use but prefer the option to carry three AAAs for a longer or less predictable trip. Petzl lists 625 lumens, an 88 g weight, a wide or mixed beam, three white levels, red continuous and strobe modes, a battery indicator, tilt plate, phosphorescent reflector, lock function, and a storage pouch that can turn the lamp into a lantern.

Skip it if you prioritize the highest published ingress rating. Petzl calls the ACTIK CORE IPX4 weather resistant; Black Diamond’s Spot 400-R is the stronger published choice for wet conditions. Confirm the current instructions and mounting limits before attaching either to specialized equipment.

How we compared

The criteria behind the pick.

We treated this as an outdoor-lighting and trip-preparation decision, not a promise that the highest lumen figure is always the right setting.

Battery flexibility is practical range

Petzl’s HYBRID CONCEPT system changes what can happen after the included rechargeable battery runs low: the ACTIK CORE can use three AAA/LR03 cells, with performance adjusted for the energy source. Black Diamond’s Spot 400-R instead uses an integrated 1500 mAh Li-ion battery. Neither system is universally better; Petzl favors contingency planning, while Black Diamond favors a single integrated setup.

Use the published ratings precisely

Black Diamond specifies IP67 and says the Spot 400-R is waterproof-tested to operate at least one meter underwater for 30 minutes. Petzl specifies IPX4 and calls the ACTIK CORE weather resistant. We preserve those maker terms rather than treating a rating as a blanket use guarantee. Keep ports closed, inspect seals, and follow the current manual.

Peak output is not the whole story

Petzl’s 625-lumen MAX POWER setting reaches a listed 115 m for 2 hours with the CORE battery, while Black Diamond lists 400 lumens, 100 m, and 4 hours on high. Petzl has the higher ceiling; Black Diamond has the longer listed high-mode runtime. For close camp tasks, red light, low modes, fit, and intuitive controls can be more important than either maximum figure.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which rechargeable headlamp is the better choice for most people?

The Petzl ACTIK CORE is the better all-around choice for most people who hike, run, or travel overnight because it produces 625 lumens, offers a wide-or-mixed beam, includes a 1250 mAh CORE battery, and can also run on three AAA batteries. Choose the Black Diamond Spot 400-R when its 86 g weight, IP67 rating, 400-lumen output, and simple integrated 1500 mAh battery better match a compact, weather-exposed camp or trail kit.

Which headlamp is brighter and reaches farther?

Petzl lists the ACTIK CORE at 625 lumens and 115 m in MAX POWER with its CORE rechargeable battery. Black Diamond lists the Spot 400-R at 400 lumens and 100 m on high. Brightness and distance help separate these models, but beam pattern and the setting you actually use matter more than the largest published number alone.

Can both headlamps use AAA batteries?

No. Petzl says the ACTIK CORE includes its CORE rechargeable battery and also works with three AAA/LR03 batteries through its HYBRID CONCEPT design. Black Diamond lists the Spot 400-R with an integrated 1500 mAh Li-ion battery and micro-USB charge port, so it is the simpler rechargeable-only option.

Which headlamp is more weather resistant?

Black Diamond lists the Spot 400-R at IP67 and says it is waterproof-tested to operate at least one meter underwater for 30 minutes. Petzl lists the ACTIK CORE at IPX4, described as weather resistant. Those are different manufacturer ratings; neither replaces sensible battery-door, charging-port, and care guidance for your exact unit.

Do both headlamps have red light and a lock?

Yes. Black Diamond lists red night vision with dimming and strobe, plus a digital lockout. Petzl lists continuous red lighting and red strobe, plus a LOCK function to prevent accidental activation during transport or storage.
