Anker is the safer default for shared desks.

The biggest practical difference is spec clarity. Anker's public spec table says every USB-C port can reach 100W as a single-port output and shows how total output behaves as more ports are used. That makes it easier to decide where a MacBook, iPad, phone, earbuds case, or handheld console should plug in.

UGREEN's Nexode 200W still covers the same broad use case: 200W total output, six total ports, four USB-C ports, two USB-A ports, GaN charging, and broad laptop/phone/tablet compatibility. The tradeoff is that its public page is less specific about per-port power sharing, so shoppers who need predictable dual-laptop charging have less to verify before checkout.

Buyer guide

Choose by port behavior, not just wattage.

A 200W number is only the start. The real buying split is whether you know how much power each port can provide, whether you still need USB-A, and whether your cables can carry the charging rate your laptop expects.

Anker Prime Charger 200W

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want any of the four USB-C ports to be usable as a 100W single-device laptop port.
  • You regularly mix USB-C laptops with USB-A accessories such as watches, earbuds, lights, or older cables.
  • You want a published multi-port output table before trusting one charger with a shared work desk.
Skip this if
  • You need a charger that includes a full set of high-watt USB-C cables for every device.
  • You want all six front ports to be USB-C with no USB-A ports taking up space.
  • Your main laptop needs more than 100W from a USB-C charger and cannot fall back gracefully.
UGREEN Nexode 200W

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want a six-port 200W charging station and appreciate getting a USB-C to USB-C cable in the box.
  • Your desk is mostly phones, tablets, a handheld console, earbuds, and one laptop rather than two high-draw laptops.
  • You need broad protocol support, including Power Delivery, Quick Charge 4, PPS, and AFC.
Skip this if
  • You need a clearly published per-port table before assigning charging spots to two laptops.
  • You assumed every connected device can receive maximum speed at once from a 200W shared charger.
  • You prefer Anker's smaller published footprint and explicitly stated weight.
200W is shared

Both chargers have a 200W total power budget. That can cover a laptop plus several smaller devices, but it does not mean six devices each receive their maximum charging rate at the same time.

Cable rating matters

For 100W charging, the USB-C cable has to support the power level. Anker specifically notes that 100W output requires an e-marker cable, and the device's own input limit still controls the final rate.

USB-A still earns space

USB-A ports are less future-proof, but they are useful for low-draw accessories and old cables. If your desk is already USB-C-only, an all-USB-C charger may be cleaner than either of these 4C/2A layouts.

Before you buy

Audit your desk before replacing every power brick.

  • List the devices that charge at the same time, then separate laptop-class loads from phones and accessories.
  • Check each laptop's USB-C charging requirement; large workstations may want more than a 100W port can provide.
  • Keep airflow around the charger and avoid burying it under papers, cable sleeves, or monitor stands.
  • Use a real surge protector or UPS upstream if you need AC surge protection; these are USB charging stations.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which 200W desktop charger is better for most desks?

Anker Prime Charger 200W is the better default for most desks because all four USB-C ports can serve as 100W single-device ports, it includes two USB-A ports, and Anker publishes clearer multi-port output behavior.

Why choose the UGREEN Nexode 200W instead?

Choose UGREEN Nexode 200W if you want a 200W six-port charger with an included USB-C to USB-C cable and you are comfortable relying on UGREEN's power-sharing behavior for a mixed phone, tablet, laptop, and accessory desk.

Can either charger give every connected device full speed at once?

No. Both are 200W total desktop chargers, so connected devices share the available power. Cable capability, device input limits, and which ports are in use all affect the actual charging rate.

Do you need special USB-C cables for 100W charging?

For 100W USB-C charging, use an e-marker cable rated for the power level. Anker specifically notes that 100W output depends on e-marker cables and the device's own input power limit.

Are these replacements for a surge protector?

No. These are desktop USB charging stations, not AC surge strips. Use a proper surge protector or UPS separately if your setup needs AC outlet protection.
