The FELCO 6 is the better pick for most people because it pairs the right default fit with the stronger ownership story. FELCO identifies it for medium right hands and publishes a 195 mm length, 210 g weight, and a 20 mm cutting diameter — a millimeter more stated capacity than the Fiskars — with forged-aluminum handles, a hardened-steel blade, a micrometric adjustment mechanism, a wire-cutting notch, and replaceable parts.

The Fiskars PowerGear2 Softgrip Pruner has the real counterargument. Fiskars lists patented PowerGear technology — modified gears and a cam mechanism that optimize power through the middle of a cut — a contoured rolling Softgrip handle, a fully hardened bypass steel blade with low-friction coating, and a 3/4-inch cutting capacity. For hands that tire or ache through a long session, that assistance is not a gimmick; it is the reason to buy the tool.

What separates them is how each one ages and fits. The FELCO 6 is sized for the most common hands and built to be adjusted, maintained, and re-parted for years. The Fiskars asks less of your grip on every cut but centers on its mechanism rather than a parts ecosystem. Most gardeners are better served by the tool that fits and lasts; gardeners whose hands are the limiting factor should take the leverage.

Fit check

Choose by what limits your session.

If the session ends because the tool is wrong for your hand size, choose fit. If it ends because your grip gives out, choose leverage.

Buy the FELCO 6 if

You want a professional-style bypass pruner in a shorter, lighter format that FELCO identifies for medium right hands. Its 195 mm length and 210 g published weight suit deadheading, tidying perennials, shaping shrubs, and making many controlled cuts in a session, and its forged construction, adjustment system, and replaceable parts suit keeping one tool for years.

Skip it if: you have large hands, your grip tires quickly through repeated cuts, or you want a leverage mechanism to do more of the work in the middle of each cut.

Buy the Fiskars if

Your recurring work is flower beds, young shrubs, green stems, and regular cleanup where branches remain within the stated 3/4-inch capacity, and your hands feel it afterward. The PowerGear2 is built around a modified-gear and cam mechanism that Fiskars says multiplies leverage, plus a contoured rolling Softgrip handle.

Skip it if: You want a forged, serviceable tool with published replacement parts and micrometric adjustment, or you routinely cut at the top of the stated range where the FELCO's 20 mm published diameter gives a little more room.

Quick fit check: Look at a normal week of trimming. If your hands are medium-sized and the work is ordinary garden maintenance, the FELCO 6's fit and serviceability are the safer default. If repeated cutting strains your grip, the Fiskars's gear assistance is the more practical tool regardless of spec-sheet pedigree.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

A bypass pruner comparison should not turn into a contest between one capacity number and another. Here the numbers are within a millimeter — 20 mm against 3/4 inch — so the decision has to come from fit, effort, and how each tool is kept over time. Plant variety, branch condition, angle, tool condition, and user technique all affect the real task.

We gave the FELCO 6 the overall edge because tool fit has to be earned on repeated cuts, and FELCO explicitly identifies this model for medium hands at a published 195 mm and 210 g. It retains the core construction and serviceability cues shoppers look for — forged-aluminum handles, hardened-steel blade, micrometric adjustment, replaceable parts — without asking the user to manage a larger handle or a mechanism.

We gave Fiskars its due in the same terms we used when it won its own comparison: the gear-and-cam arrangement optimizes power at the difficult middle of a cut, and the contoured rolling handle is designed for comfort. That is a coherent specification for repeated cleanup work, and for hands that tire, it outweighs pedigree. It is the better tool for that specific limitation, not the better default for every hand.

Which bypass pruner is the better pick for most people?+

The FELCO 6 is the better pick for most people choosing between these models. FELCO designs it for medium hands and publishes a 195 mm length, 210 g weight, 20 mm cutting diameter, forged-aluminum handles, micrometric adjustment, and replaceable parts. Choose the Fiskars PowerGear2 if hand strain on repeated cuts is your main problem and its gear-assisted leverage matters more.

Which pruner cuts larger branches?+

On published specifications they are nearly tied: FELCO lists a 20 mm maximum cutting diameter for the FELCO 6, and Fiskars lists the PowerGear2 for branches up to 3/4 inch thick. Those are stated capacity limits, not a promise that every branch or wood condition will cut the same way.

What does PowerGear2 change on the Fiskars pruner?+

Fiskars says its modified gears and cam mechanism optimize cutting power through the middle of a cut, where a branch is thickest, and describes the design as multiplying leverage. The tool also has a contoured rolling handle intended to reduce hand strain. It is the more purpose-built option when repeated lighter pruning is the normal job and your grip is the limit.

Which pruner is more serviceable long term?+

The FELCO 6. FELCO publishes replacement parts for the model, a micrometric adjustment mechanism, and a forged-aluminum, hardened-steel construction designed to be maintained. The Fiskars centers on its gear mechanism and coated blade rather than a parts ecosystem.

Which one should I choose for flowers and shrubs?+

For routine flowers, perennials, and shrub shaping with medium hands, the FELCO 6's fit and lighter published weight make it the easier default. If a long session of the same work leaves your hands sore, the Fiskars's leverage and rolling handle are the more practical choice.
