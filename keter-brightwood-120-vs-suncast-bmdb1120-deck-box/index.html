Head-to-head 120-gallon deck box comparison

Keter Brightwood 120 vs Suncast BMDB1120

Both are 120-gallon resin chests built to clear cushions, pool gear, and garden extras from the patio. Keter’s Brightwood prioritizes a single, reinforced bench-style lid and ventilation. Suncast’s BMDB1120 makes access easier with two soft-close lid panels. The better pick depends on whether you want one broad opening or easier reach from either side.

Verdict

Which one should most people buy?

The Suncast 120 Gallon Split Lid Extra Large Deck Box is the more practical choice for most patios. It matches the Brightwood’s stated 120-gallon capacity, but its two lids let you reach a smaller section without lifting the full top. Suncast also publishes a 300-pound lid weight capacity, spring shocks, soft-close action, multi-wall panels, and a 10-year limited warranty.

The Keter Brightwood is still the better match when the deck box will double as a simple bench and you prefer one broad, uninterrupted opening. Keter specifies steel-reinforced resin construction, hydraulic pistons, ventilation, carrying handles, a lockable metal latch, and a 661-pound loading capacity. Those are meaningful details for a box that stays in one place and holds bulky cushions.

Buy the Suncast unless one-piece access, Keter’s ventilated design, or the Brightwood’s bench-oriented lid is the reason you are shopping.

Fit check

Choose by access routine, not wood-look texture.

Both products are large resin deck boxes with lockable lids and maker-described weather-resistant construction. Capacity is not the decision. How you load, retrieve, and use the lid is.

Choose the Keter Brightwood if you want one clear bay.

The Brightwood makes sense when you tend to load a full stack of cushions, a cover, or other broad items at once. Its single lid gives you the whole interior opening in one move. Keter’s published ventilation also makes it the more deliberate choice for a box holding textiles between patio days. The 57-inch width is slightly shorter than Suncast’s, but its 27.5-inch depth is greater.

Skip it if: the box sits behind a sofa, against a wall, or beside a grill where opening one full lid repeatedly would be inconvenient.

Choose the Suncast if several people use the box.

The BMDB1120’s split top is the sensible everyday format. One person can lift a smaller lid panel for a towel or toy while the other half stays closed. Suncast says its spring shocks hold the lids open and its soft-close design slows their return. Its 59.25-inch width and 24.25-inch depth trade a little depth for a longer, shallower footprint.

Skip it if: you strongly prefer a single lid for loading oversized pieces or want Keter’s explicitly stated ventilated interior.

Measure the patio path first. These are assembled storage pieces, not small bins. Compare the external width, depth, and height against the final location and the route to it. Follow each manufacturer’s assembly and care instructions; this comparison does not replace them.

How we compared

The criteria behind the pick.

Equal capacity moves the decision to access.

Both makers call these 120-gallon deck boxes. That makes capacity a tie at the headline level, so the recommendation turns on the layout a person interacts with every day. A single lid is direct and roomy. Two lids are more selective. Neither approach is automatically superior, but a patio storage box is opened frequently for small things as well as loaded occasionally for big things. That favors Suncast’s split format for most households.

Published dimensions matter more than the product silhouette.

At a glance, both are long, low resin chests. Their official measurements show a meaningful shape difference. The Suncast is 2.25 inches wider, 3.25 inches shallower, and 1.55 inches taller externally. Keter has more stated interior depth, while Suncast has more stated interior width and height. Measure the actual wall, walkway, and furniture clearance instead of choosing from a photo.

Use maker language carefully.

Keter describes ventilation, steel reinforcement, an overlapping lid, and a 661-pound loading capacity. Suncast describes multi-wall panels, water resistance, UV protection, spring shocks, soft-close action, and a 300-pound lid weight capacity. Those are manufacturer claims about their respective designs, not a lab comparison by Worth Adding. We use them to identify the functional trade-off, not to make a durability or weatherproofing guarantee.

There is another distinction worth keeping in perspective: lid ratings and loading ratings are not interchangeable measures. Suncast’s published figure is specifically a lid weight capacity, while Keter calls its figure a loading capacity. The figures should not be used to declare one product stronger than the other. They are included because they are decision-relevant manufacturer specifications, and each should be read in the context of the current manual. A deck box is also not a substitute for a purpose-built safety device, outdoor seat, or weatherproof enclosure. Keep the use case ordinary: organized patio storage, reasonable loading, and the care instructions supplied by the maker.

Warranty language supports the decision, but does not settle it.

Suncast lists a 10-year limited warranty for the BMDB1120. Keter lists a 2-year limited warranty for the Brightwood. That difference is easy to notice, but it does not tell a shopper how a particular patio, contents, assembly process, or maintenance routine will perform over time. Warranty terms have exclusions and can change. The useful comparison point is that both brands publish product-specific documentation. Read it before purchase if warranty coverage is a deciding factor, then choose the lid layout that makes the box easier to use every week.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which 120-gallon deck box is the better choice for most patios?

The Suncast BMDB1120 is the more flexible everyday pick because its split top lets you open one section at a time. Choose the Keter Brightwood instead if one wide opening and its ventilated, bench-oriented design better match what you store.

Do both boxes have the same storage capacity?

Yes. Both official product pages state 120 gallons. Their internal dimensions are shaped differently, so measure your particular cushions and the available patio footprint rather than treating the gallon number as a fit guarantee.

Can either deck box be locked?

Both manufacturers describe a provision for a padlock. Keter specifies a metal latch and Suncast says the BMDB1120 is pad-lockable. A lock is not included according to the product descriptions.

Which one is easier to open when it is against a wall?

The Suncast’s two-panel lid gives you the option to lift only one side, which is usually more convenient for frequent retrieval. Confirm the manufacturer’s clearance and assembly guidance for your exact placement.

Should I choose based on warranty length alone?

No. Suncast lists a 10-year limited warranty and Keter lists a 2-year limited warranty, but warranty terms are only one part of a purchase decision. Read the current warranty documents and prioritize the lid format and footprint that fit your routine.
