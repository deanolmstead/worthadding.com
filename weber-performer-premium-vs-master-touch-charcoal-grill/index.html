Head-to-head charcoal grill comparison

Weber Performer Premium vs Master-Touch Charcoal Grill

These two 22-inch Weber charcoal grills share a kettle format and 363 square inches of primary cooking area, but they organize a backyard cookout differently. The Performer Premium turns the kettle into a wide cart with prep space. The Master-Touch keeps the footprint tighter and adds an 80-square-inch warming rack. Choose for the way you stage food, not just the diameter of the grate.

The Weber Master-Touch Charcoal Grill 22” is the more balanced choice for most shoppers choosing between these two Weber kettles. Its official specification page lists the same 22-inch diameter and 363 square inches of primary cooking area as the Performer Premium, then adds an 80-square-inch warming rack for 443 square inches of total cooking area. That arrangement makes practical sense for a backyard cook who wants to keep buns, vegetables, or finished food near the fire without immediately needing a separate surface.

Its closed dimensions are also notably narrower: Weber lists 42 inches high by 24 inches wide by 29.5 inches deep. A kettle can still be a substantial object, and storage needs room for the lid and wheels, but the Master-Touch asks for less side-to-side patio space than the cart-style alternative. That is a useful default when the grill will live beside a fence, on a modest deck, or in a garage bay shared with other gear.

The Weber Performer Premium Charcoal Grill 22” is the real alternative when the cooking station matters as much as the fire. Weber lists 47.5 inches of closed width, two tool hooks, two locking casters, two all-weather wheels, and a plated-steel hinged cooking grate. The broad cart gives a cook an immediately available place to set a tray, season food, or park tongs. It is the choice for someone who repeatedly builds a meal around the grill and would otherwise carry a folding table out each time.

This is not a claim that one grill makes better food, reaches a particular temperature, or uses less charcoal in every condition. Both are charcoal kettles with the same primary cooking area. The decision is a work-surface and footprint decision: Master-Touch for the compact grill with a warming rack; Performer Premium for the backyard station with a prep table.

Fit check

Choose by routine, not charcoal snobbery.

Put the prep work, serving flow, and storage footprint on the table before comparing familiar kettle names.

Buy the Performer Premium if

Your cooking routine needs a place to happen. A charcoal grill creates small objects that need to be set down: a tray of seasoned food, tongs, a chimney starter after it cools, a bowl of cooked vegetables, or a plate that is waiting for the next batch. The Performer Premium’s defining feature is the wide cart and prep table that keeps that work beside the 22-inch kettle. Its two tool hooks also make a repeated routine less dependent on a separate accessory rack.

It is especially sensible when the grill stays in one backyard location. Weber lists two locking casters and two all-weather wheels, so the cart can be positioned and stabilized, but its 47.5-inch closed width and 87-pound listed weight tell a different story from a compact carry-out grill. Treat it as a small outdoor station. If that station replaces a folding table you already use on every cookout, the extra size has a direct purpose.

Skip it if: the grill needs to tuck into a narrow side yard or garage slot, or if the prep table would be unused because you already cook beside an outdoor counter or movable table.

Buy the Master-Touch if

You want the familiar kettle experience without dedicating nearly four feet of side-to-side space to a cart. Weber lists the Master-Touch at 24 inches wide when closed, and that narrower profile is easier to picture along a patio edge. The primary cooking area remains 363 square inches, so choosing the compact grill does not mean moving down to a smaller main grate.

The included warming rack is the important practical differentiator. Weber lists 80 square inches there, bringing total cooking area to 443 square inches. A warming rack is not a second high-heat zone and it cannot solve every batch-cooking problem, but it offers a defined place for finished items while the main grate stays available. For a small family meal or a casual group where food comes off in stages, that may be more useful than an attached prep table.

Skip it if: you know you will routinely need a wide built-in surface for raw-food trays and prep, and you do not want to bring a separate table outside.

Quick fit check: mark out a 47.5-inch-wide rectangle where a Performer Premium would be stored, then compare it with the Master-Touch’s 24-inch closed width. Next, decide whether your missing cooking resource is a prep surface or a place to hold finished food. Those two answers usually settle this pair.

How we reasoned

The criteria behind the pick.

This comparison weighs the details that change the ownership routine before a bag of charcoal is opened: primary cooking area, warming capacity, closed dimensions, weight where published, wheels and casters, and the presence of a usable prep surface. It does not use a live price, review average, unverified temperature claim, or a claim that one kettle produces better barbecue.

Equal primary cooking area keeps the decision focused. Weber lists both models with a 22-inch diameter and 363 square inches of primary cooking area, so the choice is not simply buying more main-grate space. The Master-Touch earns the recommendation because the 80-square-inch warming rack adds a defined holding option while its 24-inch closed width makes the grill less demanding to store. That is a broad benefit for shoppers who have not explicitly said they need a cart station.

The Performer Premium earns a clear alternative position because its cart is functional, not cosmetic. The table changes where prep happens, and the hooks and wheels support a repeated outdoor-cooking setup. Its listed 87-pound weight and 47.5-inch width are not defects by themselves; they are the trade-off for a grill that is intended to occupy more permanent territory. A shopper with a roomy patio and no adjacent counter can reasonably value that station more than a warming rack.

We deliberately avoid estimating how many burgers either grill can cook, how much charcoal each uses, or what outdoor conditions they will handle. Grill loading, food size, fuel, weather, assembly, and technique vary. The official dimensions and feature lists are more dependable inputs for this specific purchase decision.

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Which Weber charcoal grill should most people buy?+

Choose the Weber Master-Touch Charcoal Grill 22” when you want a compact charcoal kettle with a built-in warming rack and a smaller closed footprint. Choose the Weber Performer Premium Charcoal Grill 22” when a fixed prep table, tool hooks, and a cart-style grilling station will make outdoor cooking easier. Both official product pages list a 22-inch diameter and 363 square inches of primary cooking area.

Do both grills have the same primary cooking area?+

Yes. Weber lists 363 square inches of primary cooking area for both the Performer Premium and Master-Touch 22-inch grills. The Master-Touch page also lists an 80-square-inch warming rack and 443 square inches of total cooking area. That extra rack is the practical capacity difference to consider when food needs a place to wait.

Which grill needs more patio space?+

The Performer Premium is the larger station. Weber lists its closed dimensions as 45 inches high by 47.5 inches wide by 28 inches deep. The Master-Touch is listed at 42 inches high by 24 inches wide by 29.5 inches deep when closed. Measure the place where you will cook and store the grill, including room to open the lid and move around it.

Is the Performer Premium easier to move?+

Neither page makes a universal portability promise. Weber lists the Performer Premium at 87 pounds and describes two locking casters plus two all-weather wheels. The Master-Touch has a smaller closed width, but a shopper should consider stairs, gates, storage routes, and the actual surface before deciding which will be easier to move.

Can either grill guarantee a particular cooking result?+

No. These are manufacturer-published feature and dimension comparisons, not a guarantee about temperature control, food results, fuel use, weather performance, or fit in every patio.
