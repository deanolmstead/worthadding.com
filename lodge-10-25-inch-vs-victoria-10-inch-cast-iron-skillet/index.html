The Lodge 10.25-Inch Cast Iron Skillet is the better pick for most people because it makes the more complete everyday case without asking a shopper to infer much. Lodge identifies the selected pan as model L8SK3, a 10.25-inch Classic Cast Iron Skillet made in the USA and seasoned with 100% natural vegetable oil. Its official listing says it works on any stovetop including induction, as well as in an oven, grill, or campfire. The listing also shows the familiar long handle, opposite assist handle, and pour spouts. That is a coherent, simple setup for someone who wants one traditional skillet to reach for repeatedly.

The Victoria 10-Inch Cast Iron Skillet has a real advantage for the buyer who values the published numbers and vessel design more than Lodge’s extra quarter inch and assist-handle format. Victoria lists 16.3 by 11 by 2.8 inch overall dimensions and a 4.8-pound weight, compared with Lodge’s listed 5.66-pound L8SK3 variant weight. Victoria also describes a deeper vessel, long handle, and three layers of kosher-certified non-GMO flaxseed-oil seasoning. That makes it the more attractive alternative for a cook who prefers its stated weight and shape.

This is not a claim that one skillet heats better, seasons faster, or suits every cooktop and recipe. Those outcomes depend on the exact food, burner, technique, and care routine. The recommendation stays with the published model details that a shopper can check before checkout.

Which cast iron skillet should most people buy?+

The Lodge 10.25-Inch Cast Iron Skillet is the better default for most shoppers comparing these two. Lodge identifies a 10.25-inch skillet, model L8SK3, made in the USA and seasoned with 100% natural vegetable oil; its product listing gives the selected variant a 5.66-pound shipping weight and shows the traditional long handle, assist handle, and pour spouts. Choose the Victoria 10-Inch Cast Iron Skillet if its listed 4.8-pound weight, 16.3 by 11 by 2.8 inch overall dimensions, deeper vessel, and flaxseed-oil seasoning better match the way you cook and store a skillet.

What is the published size and weight difference?+

Lodge calls its model a 10.25-inch skillet and lists the L8SK3 variant at 5.66 pounds. Victoria lists its 10-inch skillet at 16.3 by 11 by 2.8 inches and 4.8 pounds. The measurements are not supplied in matching formats, so they are useful as maker-published references rather than a precision claim about every interior or exterior dimension.

What are the seasoning differences?+

Lodge says its Classic Cast Iron Skillet is seasoned with 100% natural vegetable oil. Victoria says its 10-Inch Cast Iron Skillet is preseasoned with three layers of kosher-certified non-GMO flaxseed oil. Both makers describe pre-seasoned cast iron, but seasoning type is only one part of the choice; follow each maker’s care instructions for the exact pan.

Which skillet has a helper handle?+

The Lodge L8SK3 product listing shows a long handle and an assist handle on the opposite side, plus pour spouts. Victoria describes a long handle and a deeper vessel on its 10-inch skillet. A handle layout can change how a pan feels in a particular kitchen, but it is not a guarantee of comfort or control.

Are both skillets compatible with every heat source?+

Lodge says its Classic Cast Iron Skillet is compatible with any stovetop including induction, oven, grill, or campfire. Victoria lists gas, electric, ceramic, and induction stovetops, oven, grill, and campfire. Confirm the manufacturer’s instructions and the requirements of your own cooking surface before use; this comparison does not make a universal compatibility or safety guarantee.
