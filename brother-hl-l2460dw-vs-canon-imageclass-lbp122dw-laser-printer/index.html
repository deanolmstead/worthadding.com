Buyer guide

Choose by paper volume, not brand habit.

Both printers cover the basic home-office brief: black-and-white laser output, duplex printing, wired and wireless networking, and mobile print paths. The meaningful difference is how often you print enough pages for tray capacity, speed, and setup controls to matter.

Brother HL-L2460DW

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You print school packets, contracts, tax paperwork, or shipping forms in batches where 36 ppm and a 250-sheet tray reduce waiting and refills.
  • Your printer has to serve more than one device and you want Ethernet, dual-band Wi-Fi, USB, AirPrint, Mopria, Wi-Fi Direct, and Brother Mobile Connect in one setup.
  • You expect regular monthly use and want the higher official recommended monthly print volume as a cushion.
Skip this if
  • Your shelf is shallow; the Brother is 14.2 inches deep before you account for cords and paper handling space.
  • You rely on the printer panel often and would rather have a multi-line display for setup and status changes.
  • Most of your jobs are one or two pages, where Canon's faster stated first-page time may matter more than Brother's batch speed.
Canon LBP122dw

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You need a print-only black-and-white laser printer for short home-office jobs and the desk depth is tight.
  • You prefer a 5-line LCD for basic menu navigation instead of managing most settings from an app or computer.
  • Your printing is light to moderate and a 150-sheet cassette is enough between refills.
Skip this if
  • You print long packets often; the 150-sheet cassette gives you less paper cushion than Brother's 250-sheet tray.
  • You want dual-band Wi-Fi listed in the official spec sheet; Canon lists Wi-Fi b/g/n plus Wi-Fi Direct.
  • You want a higher recommended monthly print range for a busier shared printer.
Tray capacity

A 250-sheet tray can take most of a paper ream at once, while a 150-sheet cassette is fine for lighter use but asks you to refill sooner during tax season, school packets, or repeated shipping-label work.

First page vs batch speed

Canon's faster first-page spec helps when you print one page and leave. Brother's higher ppm rating matters more once a job turns into several pages or multiple copies.

Footprint

The Brother is lower but deeper and heavier. The Canon is shallower, so it is easier to place on a narrow shelf, but it gives up tray capacity to get there.

Before you buy

Check these printer-specific fit points first.

  • Leave room behind the printer for the power cable, USB or Ethernet cable, and paper access; listed dimensions are the unit size, not a full working clearance plan.
  • Confirm toner availability before you commit. Brother lists TN830 family toner and DR830 drum parts; Canon lists Toner 071 and 071 High Capacity.
  • If you print from phones, check your preferred path: Brother lists Brother Mobile Connect, AirPrint, Mopria, and Wi-Fi Direct; Canon lists Canon PRINT Business, AirPrint, Mopria, and Wi-Fi Direct.
  • Buy a monochrome laser only if black-and-white documents are the job. Neither printer is meant for color graphics or photo printing.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which printer is better for a home office?

The Brother HL-L2460DW is the better all-around home-office pick if you print batches or share the printer because it has a faster rated print speed, larger 250-sheet tray, Ethernet, dual-band wireless, USB, and a higher recommended monthly print volume.

Which printer takes up less desk depth?

Canon is shallower on paper. Canon lists the LBP122dw at 14.0 inches wide by 11.1 inches deep by 8.4 inches high, while Brother lists the HL-L2460DW at 14.0 inches wide by 14.2 inches deep by 7.2 inches high.

Do both printers support automatic two-sided printing?

Yes. Brother lists automatic duplex printing for the HL-L2460DW, and Canon lists auto 2-sided printing for the imageCLASS LBP122dw.

Which one has better connectivity?

Brother has the stronger official connectivity mix for most homes because it lists Ethernet, dual-band Wi-Fi, USB 2.0, AirPrint, Brother Mobile Connect, Mopria, and Wi-Fi Direct. Canon also covers USB, Ethernet, Wi-Fi, Wi-Fi Direct, Canon PRINT Business, AirPrint, and Mopria, but does not list dual-band Wi-Fi in the same way.

Should I buy either one for color or photo printing?

No. Both are monochrome laser printers. They make sense for black-and-white documents, labels, forms, and drafts, not color presentations or photo output.
