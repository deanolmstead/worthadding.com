The DEWALT is the safer default; the Bosch is the nicer stationary setup.

The DWS780 wins because its strengths line up with the widest group of buyers: a 15 Amp motor, 2x14 cutting capacity at 90 degrees, 7-1/2 in nested crown support, a 56 lb listed weight, and DEWALT's XPS crosscut positioning system. That combination makes it easier to use in a garage, move onto a stand, and trust for finish cuts without turning the saw station into a permanent fixture.

The GCM12SD is still the one to want if your saw lives in a fixed shop position. Bosch's Axial-Glide system is the headline: it avoids long rear rails, gives the saw a smooth pull, and helps when a bench has to sit closer to a wall. The trade-off is weight and bulk: Bosch lists 65 lb and a taller body, so it is less appealing if you routinely carry the saw to the work.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which saw is better for a serious home shop?

The DEWALT DWS780 is the easier default for most serious home shops because it combines wide 2x cutting capacity, a useful XPS cutline system, and a lower 56 lb listed weight. Choose the Bosch if the saw will live against a wall or on a dedicated bench.

Why choose the Bosch GCM12SD instead?

The Bosch is compelling when workspace depth matters. Its Axial-Glide arm is designed for smooth movement and a more compact setup than a traditional rail saw, and Bosch lists front bevel controls plus strong dust collection when used with a vacuum.

Can either saw handle tall crown and base molding?

Yes, but they emphasize the numbers differently. DEWALT lists 7-1/2 in nested crown and 6-3/4 in vertical base support, while Bosch lists crown and base capacities in several spring-angle and bevel positions. Check your molding profile before buying.

Are these too much saw for casual DIY?

Probably, if you only make occasional straight crosscuts. These are heavy 12-inch dual-bevel saws for trim, shelving, built-ins, deck boards, wide 2x material, and repeated angle work where capacity and repeatability matter.

Should I buy a stand with either one?

For long casing, baseboard, decking, or sheet-goods offcuts, yes. Either saw benefits from stable side support, and the Bosch page specifically lists Bosch miter saw stands among compatible accessories.
