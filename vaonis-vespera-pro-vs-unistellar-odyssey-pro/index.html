Vespera Pro is the better overall buy for image-first prosumers.

Both telescopes simplify the hard parts of astronomy: finding targets, tracking them, and building usable images. Vespera Pro pulls ahead for most buyers because its advantages line up with long imaging nights: 225 GB of storage, up to 11 hours of battery life, a 12.5 MP square sensor, 50 MP mosaic capture, automatic dark-frame calibration, and 16-bit export options.

Odyssey Pro is still the one to buy if you want an observing experience, not only an imaging workflow. The Nikon electronic eyepiece, Unistellar app, 5,000-plus-object catalog, and citizen-science programs give it a more guided, communal feel, but you give up Vespera Pro's battery and storage margin.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which one is better for astrophotography?

Vespera Pro is the better default for astrophotography. Its longer battery life, larger internal storage, 50 MP mosaic mode, automatic dark-frame workflow, and FITS/TIFF export support are all aimed at longer imaging sessions and cleaner file handling.

Which one is better for visual observing?

Odyssey Pro is the better fit for visual observing because it includes Nikon Eyepiece Technology. Vespera Pro is screen-based and image-first, so it is less compelling if you want the feeling of looking through the instrument.

Do these smart telescopes work without a phone or tablet?

Plan on using a phone or tablet with both. Vespera Pro depends on Vaonis' app workflow, and Odyssey Pro is also built around Unistellar's app even though it adds an electronic eyepiece for observing.

Are they good for planets as well as deep-sky objects?

Odyssey Pro is the stronger planets-and-deep-sky choice because Unistellar positions the Odyssey range around both solar-system and deep-sky observing. Vespera Pro is more naturally an automated wide-field astrophotography tool.

Should storage and battery life affect the decision?

Yes, especially for long sessions. Vespera Pro lists 225 GB of storage and up to 11 hours of battery life, while Odyssey Pro lists 64 GB and up to 5 hours. That is a meaningful convenience gap if you image often.
