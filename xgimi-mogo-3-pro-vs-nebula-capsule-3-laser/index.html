Buyer guide

Choose by power, placement, and room light.

Portable projectors are not just spec sheets. The better pick depends on whether you can plug in, where the projector will sit, how dark the room gets, whether you need HDMI, and how often it leaves the house.

XGIMI MoGo 3 Pro

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the stronger listed brightness claim for dim indoor rooms and occasional patio nights.
  • You like the built-in 130-degree stand and automatic setup tools for quick wall alignment.
  • You need Micro HDMI plus USB-C power instead of relying only on wireless streaming.
Skip this if
  • You need a projector with its own battery for movie playback away from an outlet.
  • You want the smallest cylinder-shaped projector that disappears into a backpack.
  • Your use is mostly camping or outdoor travel where power access is uncertain.
Nebula Capsule 3 Laser

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You need built-in battery playback for travel, backyard nights, or rooms without a handy outlet.
  • You want a compact cylindrical projector that is easier to carry in one hand or a small bag.
  • You prefer the long-term appeal of a laser light source in a very compact body.
Skip this if
  • You want the brighter listed output for larger images or rooms that are not fully dark.
  • You need the XGIMI's built-in stand geometry to aim at awkward wall positions.
  • You expect the built-in battery to handle long double-feature nights without external power.
Brightness decides screen size

XGIMI lists 450 ISO lumens and Nebula lists 300 ANSI lumens. The standards are different, so treat them as official claims rather than a perfect apples-to-apples lab test. In practice, both should be used in dim spaces, and XGIMI has the better brightness story.

Battery changes the use case

Nebula's built-in 52Wh battery makes it the better untethered projector. XGIMI can use USB-C PD power, including compatible power banks, but that still means planning a power source and cable.

Placement matters more than specs

XGIMI's 130-degree stand, auto focus, keystone, screen alignment, and obstacle avoidance help when you are projecting from shelves, nightstands, and odd angles. Nebula is easier to pack, but may need more attention to where it sits.

Before you buy

Plan the room before you pick the projector.

  • Decide whether you will usually have wall power nearby. That single choice may decide the XGIMI vs Nebula split.
  • Check the throw distance and where the projector can physically sit before assuming it will fill your wall or screen.
  • Expect both models to look best after sunset, with lights off, and on a flat white wall or real screen.
  • Plan audio if the projector will run in a larger room or outside; built-in speakers are convenient, not a full sound system.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which portable projector is better for most people?

XGIMI MoGo 3 Pro is the better default for most indoor portable-projector shoppers because it is brighter on paper, uses Google TV with licensed Netflix, has a built-in 130-degree stand, and includes more automated image setup features. Nebula Capsule 3 Laser is better if battery-powered portability matters more than brightness.

Does either projector have a built-in battery?

Nebula Capsule 3 Laser has a built-in 52Wh battery with up to 2.5 hours of movie playback listed by Nebula. XGIMI MoGo 3 Pro does not list an internal battery in the checked specs and instead supports USB-C PD power from a compatible adapter or power bank.

Which one is brighter?

XGIMI lists MoGo 3 Pro at 450 ISO lumens. Nebula lists Capsule 3 Laser at 300 ANSI lumens. Lumen standards are not identical, but the practical takeaway is that both still need dim rooms for best results, while the XGIMI has the stronger listed brightness claim.

Which one is easier to pack?

Nebula Capsule 3 Laser is easier to pack because Nebula lists it as a smaller-than-1-liter cylinder at about 2.1 lb / 950 g. XGIMI MoGo 3 Pro is still portable, but its 204.9 x 95 x 95 mm body and 1.1 kg weight make it less pocketable.

Should I choose by streaming platform alone?

No. Streaming support matters, but the better pick also depends on room brightness, whether you need battery playback, where the projector will sit, whether the ports fit your devices, and whether the built-in speakers are enough for your room.
