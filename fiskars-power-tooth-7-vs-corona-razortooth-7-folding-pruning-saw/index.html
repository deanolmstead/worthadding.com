Fiskars wins on the uncomplicated compact routine; Corona wins when published cutting capacity and a more purpose-built pruning blade are the point.

These are direct rivals: both are handheld 7-inch folding saws made for branches a lopper will not comfortably handle. Both give a toothed blade, a grip-oriented handle, a folding storage configuration, and a lock intended to keep the blade controlled. The meaningful differences are not cosmetic. Fiskars centers its product around a POWER TOOTH blade with razor-sharp triple-ground teeth, a folding blade that locks in two positions, and Softgrip touchpoints. Corona publishes a more detailed pruning-specific package: a curved 7-inch blade, a stated up-to-3-inch branch capacity, high-carbon Japanese SK5 steel, chrome plating, impulse-hardened teeth, a centered lock, and a compatible replacement blade.

For most gardeners, the Fiskars is the more sensible default because the basic job is usually compact occasional pruning: take the saw from storage, lock it open in the useful position, make a controlled cut, fold it closed, and put it away. Fiskars explicitly says the 7-inch blade locks in two positions for overhand cuts and undercuts, then folds and locks for transport or storage. That two-position arrangement is a concrete convenience for branches that are above shoulder height, below a shrub, or simply awkward to approach.

The Corona is the better alternative when the saw itself is an active part of the yard kit rather than an occasional backup. Corona states that its curved RS16120 blade cuts green or dry branches up to 3 inches in diameter and that the handle covers the teeth when folded. Its blade material, chrome plating, impulse-hardened teeth, and replacement AC 16120-1 point to a shopper who wants to compare the cutting tool as a durable pruning component. Buy the Fiskars unless those Corona-specific features are important enough to outweigh the simpler two-position Fiskars setup.

Fit check

Choose by pruning routine, not tool mythology.

A folding pruning saw earns its place through a repeated sequence: choose a sensible branch, set the blade lock, cut with control, fold it closed, clean it, and store it safely.

Buy Fiskars POWER TOOTH if

You want a compact saw to solve the ordinary gap between hand pruners and larger long-handled tools. Fiskars describes its 7-inch POWER TOOTH Softgrip folding saw as ideal for thick branches, with triple-ground teeth and two locking positions. Those positions are the important practical detail. They give the user a choice between an overhand-cut orientation and an undercut orientation, rather than forcing the same handle angle for every branch.

The storage story is equally direct: the blade folds and locks for transportation and storage, and the handle has Softgrip touchpoints. That makes a compelling everyday format for a garage pegboard, tote, or small shed where a saw should stay compact when it is not in use. It does not make the Fiskars a universal substitute for a lopper, pole saw, or professional arborist tool. It makes it the better choice when controlled, modest pruning is the normal use case.

Quick reality check: a folding saw is still a sharp cutting tool. Inspect the lock and blade before use, wear suitable eye and hand protection for the task, support the branch appropriately, and follow the manufacturer’s current instructions. This comparison does not replace manufacturer safety guidance.

Buy Corona RazorTOOTH if

You are choosing a folding saw for deliberate branch work and want the manufacturer to spell out the cutting package. Corona says the RS16120 has a 7-inch curved blade for green or dry branches up to 3 inches in diameter, a high-carbon Japanese SK5 steel blade, chrome plating, three-sided teeth, and impulse hardening. Those details will matter more to someone who uses a pruning saw repeatedly than to someone who only needs a compact occasional tool.

The Corona’s folded handle is designed to cover the blade teeth, and its centered lock can be activated for either hand. Corona also identifies AC 16120-1 as a replacement blade for RS16120. That service path is a genuine advantage for a buyer who would rather maintain a familiar tool than automatically replace it. Choose Corona for that defined pruning-tool specification; do not choose it solely because more feature terms sound more capable.

Corona’s up-to-3-inch figure is a maker-published product specification, not a promise about every branch, tree condition, body position, or user. Hardness, moisture, grain, access, and technique all change real cutting effort. Confirm the current manual and product details before purchase.

Quick fit check: think about the thickest branches you actually prune, whether you often need overhand or undercut access, where the saw will live between uses, and whether a replacement blade matters to you. Those answers are more useful than choosing by handle color.

How we decided

The criteria behind the pick.

We weighted the things that change a folding-saw purchase after the novelty wears off: blade length and shape, tooth and material details, a published branch-capacity figure when the maker provides one, lock behavior, closed-storage protection, handle contact, and replacement support. We did not use live price, retailer ratings, or marketplace superlatives. Neither saw wins because it has a more aggressive name; it wins only when its published format better matches a normal home-garden routine.

Fiskars takes the overall recommendation because its two locking positions, triple-ground 7-inch blade, Softgrip touchpoints, and locked storage configuration describe a clean, low-friction ownership routine. Corona gets the more specialized recommendation because its official page gives a defined up-to-3-inch branch figure, curved blade, Japanese SK5 steel, chrome plating, impulse-hardened teeth, a centered lock, and a replacement blade reference. That is a real advantage for a shopper who already knows this saw will see recurring branch work.

There is no universal winner for every tree or garden. A larger saw, lopper, pole saw, or professional assessment may be a better fit for a different task. The recommendation is deliberately limited to these two 7-inch folding models and the specifications their manufacturers publish.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which 7-inch folding pruning saw is the better choice for most gardeners?

The Fiskars POWER TOOTH 7 in is the better choice for most gardeners who want a compact folding saw for regular branch work. Fiskars specifies a 7-inch blade with triple-ground teeth, two locking positions, and a blade that locks shut for storage. Pick the Corona RazorTOOTH 7 in when its stated up-to-3-inch branch capacity, curved blade, chrome plating, centered lock, and maker-described high-carbon Japanese SK5 steel are the reason you are shopping.

What is the practical difference between the blade shapes?

Fiskars describes a POWER TOOTH blade with triple-ground teeth for cutting thick branches; its published 7-inch folding model is the straightforward compact option. Corona specifies a 7-inch curved blade. A curved pruning-saw blade can help keep the cut engaged on a branch, while the Fiskars is the simpler answer if the two-position lock and compact format are more useful than Corona’s curved-blade design. Blade shape is a workflow preference, not a guarantee of speed in every wood species.

What branch size does Corona publish for the RazorTOOTH 7 in?

Corona says its RS16120 7-inch folding saw can cut green or dry branches up to 3 inches in diameter. That published limit makes it the more explicit option for shoppers who want a stated capacity number. It does not mean every branch, tree condition, or cutting position will feel the same; follow the manufacturer’s current instructions and use a tool appropriate to the job.

Do both saws lock for storage?

Yes. Fiskars says its folding blade locks in two positions and folds for safe storage and transportation. Corona says the folded handle fully covers the blade teeth and describes an easy-to-lock folding blade. Those are valuable shared features for a saw that lives in a garden tote, garage drawer, or yard-work kit; inspect the lock before each use and keep the saw away from children.

Which saw has the clearer replacement-blade path?

Corona is the clearer choice if replaceable-blade support is part of your buying decision. Corona lists a 7-inch replacement blade, AC 16120-1, for the RS16120. Fiskars’ published page for this 7-inch POWER TOOTH folding saw emphasizes the folding mechanism, triple-ground teeth, and locking positions; verify current replacement availability directly with Fiskars before making a purchase on that basis.
