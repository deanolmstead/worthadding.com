Buyer guide

Choose by practice setup.

Both are compact 88-key digital pianos with built-in speakers, headphone practice, included basics, and optional stand/pedal upgrades. The decision is mostly key feel and wireless learning tools versus lighter carry and wired USB audio.

Yamaha P-143

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the lighter piano in this comparison for moving between a bedroom, living room, closet, or lessons.
  • You plan to connect by cable and value Yamaha's listed USB audio interface for recording or app use.
  • You want a simple 10-voice practice piano with Duo mode, metronome, transpose, tuning, and fewer app distractions.
Skip this if
  • You want textured keys with escapement for a more acoustic-piano-like beginner action.
  • You want Bluetooth MIDI for wireless practice apps or a cleaner tablet setup.
  • You expect to use many onboard tones or want the higher 96-voice polyphony listed by Roland.
Roland FP-10

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the richer learning feel of PHA-4 Standard keys with escapement and Ivory Feel texture.
  • You want Bluetooth MIDI and Roland Piano App control for lessons, metronome settings, and app-based practice.
  • You care about 96-voice polyphony, 36 total tones, Twin Piano mode, and a piano that can stay useful after the first months.
Skip this if
  • You need the lighter board because you move the piano often or store it after every practice session.
  • You specifically want Yamaha's USB audio interface support for a direct wired audio workflow.
  • You are buying for a very simple, no-wireless home setup where Yamaha's smaller feature set is a benefit.
Keyboard action

Action matters more than tone count for most beginners. Roland's PHA-4 Standard with escapement and Ivory Feel is the reason the FP-10 is the default pick for learners focused on technique.

Connectivity

Yamaha's USB audio interface is useful for direct wired recording. Roland's Bluetooth MIDI is useful for practice apps and cable-light lessons, but Roland notes Bluetooth can vary by country.

Portability

A few pounds matter if the piano lives under a bed or in a closet between sessions. Yamaha is the easier lift; Roland asks you to accept more weight for the key action and wireless MIDI.

Before you buy

Check these digital piano fit points first.

  • Budget for a stable stand and bench if you do not already have them; tabletop practice can put wrists and shoulders in a poor position.
  • Check headphone plug size and adapter needs before buying for quiet practice.
  • Confirm the app workflow on your phone or tablet, especially if Bluetooth MIDI or USB app control is part of the decision.
  • Plan pedal upgrades early if half-pedaling matters for lessons; both ecosystems list optional pedals beyond the basic included switch.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which digital piano is better for most beginners?

The Roland FP-10 is the better default for most beginners who care about a more acoustic-feeling action, Bluetooth MIDI practice apps, and a stronger long-term learning feel. The Yamaha P-143 is better if you want the lighter instrument, USB audio interface support, and a simpler control set.

Does either piano have Bluetooth audio?

Roland lists Bluetooth MIDI for the FP-10, with availability depending on country, and Yamaha lists no Bluetooth on the P-143 spec sheet. Neither official spec page should be treated as promising Bluetooth audio playback for this head-to-head.

Which one is easier to move around?

The Yamaha P-143 is easier to move because Yamaha lists it at 24 lb 8 oz, while Roland lists the FP-10 at 27 lb 13 oz with the music rest detached. Both still need a stand, bench, power adapter, and pedal planning.
