Which one should most people buy?

Choose Philips Sonicare 4100 as the lower-friction default. Choose Oral-B iO Series 2 when its round brush format and three mode choices are the features you will actually use every day.

Best electric toothbrush pick

Philips Sonicare 4100, HX3681/23

View on Amazon

Sonicare wins for a predictable starter routine; Oral-B wins for round-head preference and mode flexibility.

Neither is a no-feedback budget brush. Philips says its pressure sensor detects excess pressure, gives a pulsing reminder, and automatically reduces vibration; Oral-B says its automatic sensor slows speed and signals red when brushing too hard. Both pair that feedback with a two-minute timer and 30-second zones.

The practical difference is how each routine is presented. The Sonicare 4100 keeps its published options to two intensity settings and gives a concrete 14-day battery estimate based on two two-minute brushings daily. Oral-B iO Series 2 offers super-sensitive, gentle, and daily-clean modes plus a dentist-inspired round head. Oral-B calls its battery long-lasting but does not publish a comparable day count on the official product page, so it is not a fair numeric comparison.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which is better for most people?

Philips Sonicare 4100 is the better broad-use starter pick because it pairs pressure feedback and timer guidance with a published 14-day battery estimate and two simple intensity choices. Choose Oral-B iO Series 2 if a round head and three mode choices better match your brushing preference.

Do both have pressure sensors?

Yes. Philips says the 4100 detects excess pressure, warns the user, and reduces vibration. Oral-B says the iO2 automatic pressure sensor slows speed and signals red when you brush too hard.

Do both guide a two-minute routine?

Yes. Philips lists a two-minute SmarTimer plus 30-second QuadPacer. Oral-B lists a two-minute timer with alerts every 30 seconds to change zones.

Which has better battery life?

Philips publishes a 14-day estimate based on two daily two-minute standard-mode brushings. Oral-B calls the iO2 battery long-lasting and includes a low-charge indicator, but its official product page does not publish a comparable day count. That makes Sonicare the clearer pick for shoppers who want a stated estimate, not a proven universal battery-life winner.
