Breville Bambino vs De'Longhi Dedica Arte

Breville Bambino is the better overall buy for most home baristas.

The deciding difference is the basket. The Bambino ships with both dual-wall (pressurized) and single-wall (non-pressurized) baskets in 1 and 2 cup sizes, so it can start forgiving with pre-ground coffee and then switch to the single-wall baskets as your grinder and technique improve. The Dedica Arte ships with pressurized baskets only, plus an ESE pod filter. Pressurized baskets are genuinely easier, but they are also a ceiling: extraction is managed by the basket, not by your grind, and there is no non-pressurized path to grow into.

On the published numbers the Bambino also heats faster and holds more. Its ThermoJet system is ready in 3 seconds against about 40 seconds for the Dedica Arte's thermoblock, its tank holds 47 fl oz against 35 fl oz, and its PID temperature control with low-pressure pre-infusion is the more complete extraction package on paper. Both machines use a 15 bar pump rating and a manual steam wand, so milk texturing is a hands-on skill either way.

The Dedica Arte's case is physical. At 5.9 inches wide and 8 lb it is the slimmer, lighter machine, its adjustable double drip tray fits taller cups, its 3-level temperature control is a real adjustment, and the ESE pod filter covers mornings when grinding is not happening. If the counter gap is genuinely under 6 inches, or pods are the plan, it is the right pick. For everyone else learning espresso, the Bambino is the better buy.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Which machine heats up faster?

The Breville Bambino, by a wide margin. Its ThermoJet heating system is ready in 3 seconds. The De'Longhi Dedica Arte's thermoblock takes about 40 seconds to reach brewing temperature.

Which machine is better for learning espresso?

The Breville Bambino. It includes single-wall (non-pressurized) baskets alongside the dual-wall set, a 54 mm portafilter, PID temperature control, and low-pressure pre-infusion, so your grind and technique actually drive the extraction. The Dedica Arte's pressurized baskets are easier but cap that growth.

Can the De'Longhi Dedica Arte use ESE pods?

Yes. The Dedica Arte includes an ESE pod filter alongside its pressurized single and double baskets for ground coffee. The Breville Bambino is ground-coffee only, using its single-wall and dual-wall baskets.

Which machine takes up less counter space?

The De'Longhi Dedica Arte, narrowly. It is 5.9 inches wide and weighs 8 lb, against the Breville Bambino at 6.1 inches wide and 10.42 lb. Both are compact machines; the Dedica Arte is the pick when the gap is genuinely under 6 inches.

Do either of these machines have a built-in grinder?

No. Neither the Breville Bambino nor the De'Longhi Dedica Arte includes a grinder, so budget for a separate burr grinder if you want to use fresh-ground coffee — the Bambino's single-wall baskets in particular need one.
