Winix 5500-2 is the better overall buy for most rooms.

Start with the evidence standard, because the two coverage claims are not measured the same way. The Winix is AHAM Verified for a 360 sq. ft. room, with CADR figures of 243 for dust, 246 for pollen, and 232 for smoke. The Shark's headline figure — up to 1,000 sq. ft. — is Shark's own statement, and Shark bases it on just 1 air change per hour, a much gentler standard than the AHAM room sizing. Shark publishes no AHAM Verified CADR for the HP201, so its bigger number cannot be compared like-for-like, and on the only verified figures in this pairing the Winix is the one that can be sized to a room with confidence.

The Winix also brings the stronger odor toolkit. Its AOC carbon filter is a washable bed of activated carbon granules ahead of the True HEPA filter, its Smart Sensor reacts to odorous gas, and PlasmaWave adds a switchable extra stage. The Shark's NanoSeal filter is a single replaceable multi-filter with an antimicrobial coating — capable on particles, capturing 99.98% of 0.1 to 0.2 micron particles — but there is no separate washable carbon stage, and the whole cartridge is replaced every 6 to 12 months.

The Shark's real counterargument is sensing and scale. Clean Sense IQ tracks PM1, PM2.5, and PM10, adjusts the fan on its own, and shows air quality in real time — feedback the Winix's odor-led auto mode does not match — and for a genuinely large living room the HP201 is the only one of the two even claiming that footprint. It is also the current model, while the 5500-2 is a long-running design Winix is replacing with the newer 5510. For a medium room, though, verified cleaning, odor hardware, and quieter published noise make the Winix the safer buy while it lasts.

Both units clean with True HEPA filters and auto modes. The split is whether you size the purifier by independently verified CADR or by the largest stated coverage, and whether odors or particle feedback matter more.

Buy Winix 5500-2 if

Your room is medium-sized, odors from pets, cooking, or smoke are part of the complaint, and you want cleaning figures that were independently verified — plus a washable carbon filter and a remote control instead of an app.

Buy Shark HP201 if

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Whose coverage figure can be verified?

The Winix 5500-2's. It is AHAM Verified for a 360 sq. ft. room with CADR figures of 243 for dust, 246 for pollen, and 232 for smoke. The Shark HP201's up to 1,000 sq. ft. figure is Shark's own statement, based on 1 air change per hour, and Shark publishes no AHAM Verified CADR for it — so the two numbers are not measured the same way.

Which one is better for odors and pet smells?

The Winix 5500-2. Its AOC carbon filter is a washable bed of activated carbon granules, its Smart Sensor detects odorous gas to drive the auto mode, and PlasmaWave adds a switchable ionization stage aimed at odors and chemical vapors. The Shark relies on its single NanoSeal multi-filter cartridge.

Which one shows you your air quality?

The Shark HP201. Clean Sense IQ measures PM1, PM2.5, and PM10, adjusts the fan automatically, and reports air quality in real time on the unit. The Winix's auto mode is driven by an odor sensor with a color indicator, not a particle readout.

What filters does each one need?

The Shark uses one replaceable NanoSeal HEPA multi-filter, replaced every 6 to 12 months. The Winix spreads the work: a permanent washable pre-filter cleaned about every two weeks, a washable AOC carbon filter cleaned about every three months and replaced at roughly 12 months, and a True HEPA Filter H that is replaced — never washed — on about the same yearly cycle.

Is PlasmaWave an ionizer, and can I turn it off?

Yes to both. PlasmaWave is Winix's ionization stage, and it has its own button on the unit and the remote, so it can be switched off and left off. The remaining three stages still filter mechanically — but part of the Winix's odor pitch goes with the switch.
