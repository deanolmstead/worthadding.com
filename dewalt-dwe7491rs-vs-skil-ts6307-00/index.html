Buyer guide

Match the saw to your cuts, not just the blade size.

These are both 10-inch, 15-amp portable saws. The buying decision comes down to the material you rip most often, how often you move the saw, and how much folded storage matters.

DEWALT DWE7491RS

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You regularly rip shelving, trim stock, deck boards, or sheet goods where the 32-1/2 inch right rip gives you more breathing room.
  • The saw will move between jobs, driveways, or work zones and a rolling stand is worth more than a smaller folded package.
  • You want a site-ready storage system for the guard, fence, riving knife, miter gauge, wrenches, push stick, and cord.
Skip this if
  • You mostly cut smaller project lumber in a garage and do not want a 90 lb saw package taking up floor space.
  • Your top priority is folding the saw into the smallest possible storage position after occasional weekend use.
  • You are buying your first saw for light projects and would rather put more budget toward blades, dust collection, and work support.
SKIL TS6307-00

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You need a real 10-inch table saw for DIY projects, but the saw has to fold and store between uses.
  • Most of your cuts are trim, dimensional lumber, small panels, or furniture parts rather than frequent full-width sheet rips.
  • You like the idea of rack-and-pinion fence rails and parallel blade adjustment in a simpler, lighter-use package.
Skip this if
  • You often need more than 25-1/2 inches of right-side rip capacity for cabinets, built-ins, shelving, or site work.
  • You want a rolling stand that can move around a jobsite without lifting or awkwardly carrying the saw.
  • You expect the table saw to be a daily production tool instead of an occasional shop or home-improvement saw.
Rip capacity

The DEWALT has 32-1/2 inches of right rip capacity versus 25-1/2 inches on the SKIL. That gap matters most when you are breaking down wider sheet goods or trimming large panels without a track saw.

Stand design

A rolling stand helps when the saw travels or shifts around a work area. Integrated folding legs are better when the saw lives against a wall, under a bench, or in a small garage.

Cut depth

SKIL lists a deeper 3.5 inch cut at 0 degrees, while DEWALT lists 3-1/8 inches. Both handle common 2x material, but blade setup, work support, and safe feed technique matter more than chasing the biggest number.

Before you buy

Plan the whole table-saw setup.

  • A portable table saw still needs infeed and outfeed support for long boards or sheet goods; the stand alone is not the whole work surface.
  • Budget for a better blade if your cuts involve plywood, finish trim, hardwood, or furniture parts instead of rough construction lumber.
  • Dust collection is not optional indoors. Match the saw port or elbow to a shop vacuum or dust extractor before the first messy project.
  • Use the included guard, riving knife, push stick, and anti-kickback setup. A table saw is useful because it is repeatable, not because it rewards rushing.

FAQ

Questions before you cut.

Which table saw is better for full sheets of plywood?

The DEWALT is the better choice. Its 32-1/2 inch right rip capacity gives more room for wider rips than the SKIL TS6307-00, which tops out at 25-1/2 inches to the right of the blade.

Is the SKIL TS6307-00 enough for home projects?

Yes, for many homeowners. It has a 10-inch blade, 15 amp motor, rack-and-pinion fence rails, 25-1/2 inch right rip capacity, and folding legs. It is a strong fit for trim, dimensional lumber, smaller panels, and garage projects.

Do both saws include stands?

Yes. The DEWALT DWE7491RS includes a rolling stand. The SKIL TS6307-00 has integrated folding legs that form its stand. The difference is how they move and store, not whether you get support at all.

Which one is easier to store?

The SKIL is easier to make small between projects because the legs fold into the saw package. The DEWALT rolls more conveniently, but its larger rolling-stand setup is meant for capacity and jobsite movement rather than minimal storage.

Should a beginner buy either saw?

A careful beginner can buy either, but they should also budget for work support, a good blade, hearing and eye protection, dust collection, and time to learn safe setup. The SKIL is the less intimidating ownership fit; the DEWALT is the better long-term site tool.
