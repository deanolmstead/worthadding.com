CCG-190 wins on carry and space; AKORN Jr. wins when the cook is built around controlled heat.

No pair in this group disagrees more about what a portable grill is. The AKORN Jr. is a kamado that happens to be small: triple-walled insulation, dual dampers, a cast-iron grate, and the group's only stated temperature range, 200–700°F. The CCG-190 is a 14-inch kettle that happens to weigh almost nothing: 4.2 listed pounds, three lid locks, and a dual-vent firebox.

The numbers frame the choice plainly. The Cuisinart lists more cooking area (196 square inches against 155) at about an eighth of the weight. For the trips portable grills actually take — across a park, down to a beach, from the car to a campsite — that is not a marginal difference; it is the difference between carrying the grill without thinking and planning the trip around it.

The AKORN Jr. remains the right answer for a specific cook: someone whose portable sessions are low-and-slow or carefully temperature-managed, and who mostly moves the grill from a garage to a patio. If that is your routine, buy the kamado. For portable grilling as most people practice it, the CCG-190's carry and space win.

Fit check

Choose by which extreme is yours.

A portable charcoal grill earns its place through the recurring sequence: load it, light it, arrange the food, manage heat, deal with ash after it cools, and put it away. These two each sacrifice a different step on purpose.

Buy Char-Griller AKORN Jr. if

You want compact charcoal cooking to include deliberate low-and-slow sessions as well as direct grilling. Char-Griller publishes a 200–700°F range, triple-walled insulation, and dual adjustable dampers — the core reasons to select this model, and more useful to a cook who manages airflow over time than to someone whose typical outing is a quick grill-and-go meal.

The AKORN Jr. also gives you a cast-iron cooking grate, an EasyDump ash pan, and side handles. Its compact width and depth can make storage easier, while its published 35.7-pound weight is a reason to pause before treating it as an effortless travel grill. Pick it when the cooking justifies the carrying.

Quick reality check: the AKORN Jr. is a charcoal grill with real mass. Plan for a two-handed lift, a stable outdoor surface, adequate clearance, and a full cool-down before it goes back in the vehicle.

Buy Cuisinart CCG-190 if

Your portable grill genuinely gets carried — across a park, down a beach path, or from a campsite parking area to the site. At a listed 4.2 pounds with three secure lid locks, the CCG-190 is the one grill in this group you can move one-handed without planning around it, and its 196-square-inch grate still fits a small group's burgers and dogs.

The trade is cooking sophistication: a chrome-plated grate, dual vents, and no insulation or stated temperature range. Pick it when the walk is the constraint and the cooking is direct and simple.

Quick reality check: the CCG-190 is not a low-and-slow tool. If controlled-temperature charcoal cooking is on your agenda, this is the wrong grill no matter how light it is.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which portable charcoal grill is the better pick for most people?

The Cuisinart CCG-190. It lists more cooking area (196 square inches) than the AKORN Jr. (155) at a listed 4.2 pounds against 35.7 — the lightest carry in this group. Choose the Char-Griller AKORN Jr. when its insulated kamado body, 200–700°F stated range, cast-iron grate, and low-and-slow airflow control are the reason you are shopping.

How big is the weight difference?

About eight to one: Cuisinart lists the CCG-190 at 4.2 pounds; Char-Griller lists the AKORN Jr. at 35.7 pounds. That is the widest carry gap in the portable charcoal group, and it reflects a real format difference — a thin 14-inch kettle against an insulated kamado.

Which one is better for low-and-slow cooking?

The AKORN Jr., clearly. Its triple-walled insulation, dual dampers, and stated 200–700°F range are published for exactly that. The CCG-190 is a simple direct-grilling portable with dual vents and no insulation.

Which one has more cooking space?

The CCG-190: 196 listed square inches against the AKORN Jr.'s 155. Both are round grates, so the difference is straightforward — about a quarter more room on the Cuisinart.

Which one handles ash better?

Both address it: the AKORN Jr. has an EasyDump ash pan, and the CCG-190 has a mess-free ash base. Either way, let ash cool completely before handling, and follow the manufacturer's instructions for disposal.
