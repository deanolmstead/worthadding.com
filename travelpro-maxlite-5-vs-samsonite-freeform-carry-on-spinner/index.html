Buyer guide

Buy this if, skip this if.

The right carry-on depends on your packing habits as much as the shell. Weight, width, exterior pockets, and expansion behavior change how the bag feels on a real travel day.

Travelpro Maxlite 5

Buy this if
  • You want the lighter bag before clothes, shoes, and toiletries are added.
  • You use exterior pockets for a charger, documents, glasses, or last-minute airport items.
  • You usually fly domestic routes and want a 9 in published depth before using expansion.
Skip this if
  • You want the cleaner wipe-down exterior and corner rigidity of a hardside shell.
  • You prefer an integrated lock instead of adding your own luggage lock.
  • You overpack against the expansion zipper and often fly stricter international carriers.

Samsonite Freeform

Buy this if
  • You want a hardside polypropylene shell with a recessed TSA combination lock.
  • You pack in two structured halves and use a divider, cross ribbons, and interior pockets.
  • You prefer a smoother exterior without softside front pockets catching on tight spaces.
Skip this if
  • You want the lightest bag here for overhead-bin lifting.
  • You need front-pocket access for items after the suitcase is closed.
  • Your most-used airline is strict about 10 in carry-on depth or 15 in width.
Spec meaning

The 1.1 lb weight gap matters when packed.

Travelpro's 5.4 lb listed weight gives you more of your lift and airline-weight allowance for the actual contents. That is the biggest everyday advantage if you pack dense shoes, books, cameras, or work gear.

Spec meaning

Overall dimensions are not the same as case dimensions.

Both brands publish exterior sizes that include wheels and handles. Travelpro also lists smaller case dimensions, which helps explain why a 21 in bag can still have a 23 in overall height.

Spec meaning

Expansion is useful, but it can change airline fit.

The Maxlite 5 has more listed expansion than the Freeform, but Travelpro itself warns that a fully expanded carry-on may not meet airline restrictions. Treat expansion as a return-trip overflow feature.

Before you buy

Check your real route, not a generic carry-on rule.

  • Measure including wheels, handles, bulging pockets, and expansion.
  • Use your most-flown airline's posted carry-on limits before relying on any product label.
  • Softside pockets help access, but they can bulge beyond the listed depth when stuffed.
  • Hardside suitcases keep a cleaner shape, but they usually need more floor space to open fully.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which carry-on spinner is better for most people?

The Travelpro Maxlite 5 Carry-On Spinner is the better default for most domestic carry-on shoppers because it is lighter, has exterior pockets, lists 46 liters of volume, and keeps the overall depth at 9 inches before expansion. The Samsonite Freeform is better if you want a hardside polypropylene shell, recessed TSA lock, and structured divider.

Which one is lighter?

Travelpro lists the Maxlite 5 Carry-On Spinner at 5.4 pounds. Samsonite lists the Freeform Carry-On Spinner at 6.5 pounds. That 1.1-pound difference matters most when lifting a packed bag into an overhead bin.

Which one is better for overhead-bin fit?

Both brands publish 23-inch overall exterior heights including wheels and handles, so shoppers should still check the airline they fly most. Travelpro lists a 14.5-inch width and 9-inch depth, while Samsonite lists 15-inch width and 10-inch depth.
