Head-to-head robot vacuum comparison

Roborock Q5 Pro vs iRobot Roomba j7

Roborock Q5 Pro and iRobot Roomba j7 take opposite bets on what makes a robot vacuum good. The Roborock is the hardware play: a published 5,500 Pa suction figure, DuoRoller dual rubber brushes, LiDAR mapping, a 770 ml dustbin, up to 240 minutes of runtime, and a snap-on mop. The Roomba is the awareness play: a PrecisionVision camera that identifies and steers around cords and pet waste, backed by iRobot's Pet Owner Official Promise — with a relative suction claim and about 75 minutes of runtime.

Roborock Q5 Pro is the better overall buy for most rooms.

Start with the cleaning hardware, because only one of these brands publishes its numbers. Roborock rates the Q5 Pro at 5,500 Pa of suction and pairs it with DuoRoller dual rubber brushes, claiming a 20% better hair pickup rate and fewer tangles. iRobot publishes no suction figure in pascals for the j7 at all — only that its Power-Lifting Suction is 10x that of the entry-level Roomba 600 series — alongside dual rubber brushes of its own and an Edge-Sweeping Brush. The j7 cleans well in practice, but on the only published figures in this pairing, the Roborock is the stronger, bigger cleaner.

Stamina widens the gap. The Q5 Pro runs up to 240 minutes per charge and is rated for up to 3,767 sq. ft. in Quiet mode, with a 770 ml dustbin that swallows long pet hair between empties. The j7's battery lasts about 75 minutes per the listing data, so larger homes lean on Smart Recharge and Resume — the robot tops up mid-job and picks up where it left off, which works but stretches the total clean. The Roborock also mops as it goes, with a 180 ml SnapMop tank; the j7 is vacuum-only, and mopping means adding a separate Braava jet m6 through Imprint Link.

The Roomba's counterargument is the best one in this class: awareness. PrecisionVision uses a front camera to identify charging cords, socks, shoes, and pet waste and steer around them, and iRobot guarantees the pet-waste part with its Pet Owner Official Promise — if the j7 runs over solid pet waste in its first year, iRobot replaces it. The Q5 Pro's LiDAR maps your rooms precisely but identifies nothing in them; it will bump a cord and has no way to spot an accident before it reaches it. If your floors regularly hold that kind of obstacle, the j7's camera can outweigh every spec above. For most homes, though, published cleaning power, runtime, and bin size win the day.

Fit check

Choose by what stops your current robot: weak cleaning or blind navigation.

Both map your home and clean room by room from an app. The split is whether the robot's problem is pickup power and stamina, or the obstacles it keeps running into.

Buy Roborock Q5 Pro if

Your floors are mostly clear, and you want the strongest published suction, LiDAR room mapping, a big bin, marathon runtime, and light mopping in the same pass — especially with pet hair on carpet.

Skip it if your floors regularly hold charging cords, socks, or pet accidents you need the robot to see and avoid, or you want a self-emptying dock at this model — that is the Q5 Pro+.

Buy iRobot Roomba j7 if

Your home has pets and the clutter that comes with them — cords, toys, the occasional accident — and you want the robot to identify and avoid obstacles, with cleaning scheduled around your routine by iRobot OS.

Skip it if you want the strongest published suction figure, a long single-run runtime for a big home, or any mopping from the same robot.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Answers match the FAQ structured data on this page.

Which one actually avoids pet waste and cords?

The iRobot Roomba j7. Its PrecisionVision camera navigation identifies charging cords, socks, shoes, and pet waste and steers around them, and iRobot backs pet-waste avoidance with its Pet Owner Official Promise. The Roborock Q5 Pro's LiDAR navigation maps rooms precisely but does not identify obstacles, so it can bump into a cord and cannot spot pet waste before reaching it.

Which one cleans better on carpet and pet hair?

On published figures, the Roborock Q5 Pro. Roborock rates it at 5,500 Pa of suction with DuoRoller dual rubber brushes and claims a 20% better hair pickup rate with fewer tangles. iRobot publishes no pascal figure for the j7 — only a relative claim of 10x the Power-Lifting Suction of the Roomba 600 series — so the Roborock's cleaning hardware is the one that can be compared on paper.

Which one runs longer and covers a bigger home?

The Roborock Q5 Pro, by a wide margin. It runs up to 240 minutes per charge and is rated for up to 3,767 sq. ft. in Quiet mode, with a 770 ml dustbin. The Roomba j7 lasts about 75 minutes per the listing data and relies on Smart Recharge and Resume to top up and continue on larger homes.

Does either one mop or empty itself?

The Q5 Pro mops — its 180 ml SnapMop tank lets it vacuum and mop in one pass — but the base model does not empty itself; self-emptying is the separate Q5 Pro+. The Roomba j7 does neither: it is vacuum-only, and self-emptying belongs to the separate j7+ with its Clean Base.

Do both robots need an app and WiFi?

Yes, both are app-led. The Q5 Pro connects over 2.4 GHz WiFi only, and Roborock states the product will not function as intended without the Roborock app. The j7 runs on iRobot OS through the iRobot Home app and also works with Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri voice control.
