Buyer guide

Choose by your actual prep routine.

Full-size food processors earn their counter space when you repeat the same prep jobs often. Decide around bowl size, motor confidence, slicing control, storage, weight, and cleanup friction.

Cuisinart Custom 14

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You process large batches often and want the extra headroom of a 14-cup bowl.
  • You knead dough, shred firm cheese, make dips, or prep dense vegetables often enough to care about the 720-watt motor and longer motor warranty.
  • You prefer simple controls and fixed-purpose blades over more adjustable mechanisms.
Skip this if
  • You have tight cabinet space and want blades and discs stored inside the bowl.
  • You want external slicing-thickness control without swapping technique or discs.
  • You need a lighter appliance that is easy to move in and out for small jobs.
KitchenAid 13-Cup

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the blades and discs organized in an in-bowl storage caddy instead of loose accessories.
  • You slice produce often and want the externally adjustable Exact Slice disc.
  • You value lighter handling, a twist-free bowl setup, and a 3-in-1 feed tube for different ingredient sizes.
Skip this if
  • You want the biggest bowl in this matchup for batch prep.
  • You want the stronger published motor rating and longer motor warranty coverage.
  • You mostly do heavy dough or dense shredding jobs where the Cuisinart workhorse design is the safer match.
One cup changes batch flow

A 14-cup bowl does not sound much larger than 13 cups, but the extra space helps when cabbage, shredded cheese, pie dough, salsa, or chopped vegetables climb the bowl wall.

Storage affects whether you use it

KitchenAid keeping accessories in the work bowl can matter more than a spec sheet if your processor lives in a cabinet. Loose discs and stems are one of the reasons big processors sit unused.

Slicing control is a real workflow choice

Cuisinart includes a fixed 4 mm slicing disc. KitchenAid gives you external thickness adjustment, which is more useful if you switch between potatoes, cucumbers, onions, and cabbage in the same week.

Before you buy

Think past the first recipe.

  • Measure both height and storage space with accessories included. A processor that is annoying to store usually gets used less.
  • Decide whether dough is a real priority. Heavy dough work favors motor headroom and warranty confidence over convenience features.
  • Do not buy only for bowl capacity if you usually prep small amounts. Big bowls can be awkward for tiny sauces or a single onion.
  • Check your cleanup tolerance. Dishwasher-safe removable parts help, but sharp discs and large bowls still reward immediate rinsing.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which food processor is better for most serious home cooks?

Cuisinart Custom 14 is the better default for frequent prep because it has the larger bowl, stronger published motor rating, simpler workhorse setup, and longer motor warranty coverage.

Which one is easier to store?

KitchenAid 13-Cup is the easier-storage pick because its accessories fit into an in-bowl storage caddy. That matters if the machine lives in a cabinet and you dislike loose discs, stems, and blades.

Which is better for slicing vegetables?

KitchenAid gets the edge for slicing flexibility because it uses an externally adjustable Exact Slice disc. Cuisinart includes a stainless 4 mm slicing disc, which is simple but less flexible.

Which one is better for dough?

Cuisinart is the safer choice if dough is a frequent job because the larger bowl, 720-watt motor, and longer motor warranty are better aligned with heavy prep. KitchenAid does include a dough blade, but convenience is its stronger pitch.

Should I buy based on wattage alone?

No. Wattage matters, but bowl geometry, blade setup, feed tube design, storage, cleanup, warranty, and whether you enjoy using the machine matter just as much in daily cooking.
