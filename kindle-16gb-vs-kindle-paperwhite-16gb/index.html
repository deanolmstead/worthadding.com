Which one should most people buy?

Paperwhite is the stronger everyday e-reader for adults who read often, especially at night or near water. The basic Kindle is still the cleaner choice for the smallest bag, lighter hand feel, and simplest reading setup.

Buy the Paperwhite unless portability is the main reason you are buying.

The Paperwhite wins because its upgrades affect ordinary reading, not just spec-sheet bragging. The larger display gives each page more room, the warm-light adjustment is easier for evening sessions, the battery rating is longer, and waterproofing makes bath, pool, beach, and spill-prone reading less stressful.

Choose the Kindle 16 GB when the device needs to disappear into a purse, jacket pocket, sling, or commute bag. Its 6-inch 300 ppi display is still sharp, its 5.56-ounce listed weight is meaningfully lighter, and its six-week battery estimate is plenty for casual reading.

Buyer guide

Choose by reading routine.

These Kindles share the same core store and 16 GB storage tier, so the decision is mostly physical: screen size, hand weight, lighting, waterproofing, and how long you read per session.

Kindle 16 GB

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the smallest and lightest current Kindle for commuting, purse carry, travel, or one-hand reading.
  • You read mostly in shorter sessions and are satisfied with a 6-inch 300 ppi black-and-white display.
  • You do not need waterproofing, amber warm-light control, or a larger page layout.
Skip this if
  • You read for long stretches and would benefit from the larger 7-inch Paperwhite screen.
  • You read in bed often and want warmer lighting instead of only a standard adjustable front light.
  • You plan to read near a bathtub, pool, beach bag, or kitchen counter where waterproofing matters.
Kindle Paperwhite 16 GB

Buy this if / skip this if

Buy this if
  • You want the better everyday reading device and care more about comfort than the smallest possible body.
  • You read at night and want the display to shift from white to amber for a softer bedside setup.
  • You want IPX8 waterproofing for bath, pool, beach, travel, or spill-prone reading spots.
Skip this if
  • You strongly prefer the lightest one-hand device and do not want a 7.4-ounce reader.
  • Your reading is mostly short, occasional, and away from water.
  • You only need the core Kindle experience and would rather keep the device as compact as possible.
Screen size

The jump from 6 inches to 7 inches is useful if you raise font size, read longer books, or dislike frequent page turns. If pocketable carry matters more, the smaller Kindle wins.

Battery claims

Amazon lists up to 6 weeks for the Kindle and up to 12 weeks for the Paperwhite, both based on half an hour of reading per day with wireless off and specific light settings.

Waterproofing

Paperwhite's IPX8 rating is a practical ownership advantage if the reader will live near water. If you only read on a couch, train, or desk, that may not justify the larger body.

Before you buy

Check these e-reader fit points first.

  • Choose the lockscreen-ad option intentionally; it can change the checkout configuration even when the hardware is otherwise the same.
  • Think about hand fatigue: the Paperwhite has the more comfortable screen, but the Kindle is meaningfully lighter.
  • Library borrowing, Audible support, and personal-document workflows can vary by region, account, and format, so confirm your actual reading sources before switching ecosystems.
  • Buy the case around the exact model generation. Current Kindle and Paperwhite bodies are different sizes, and older cases often do not fit cleanly.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which Kindle is better for most readers?

The Kindle Paperwhite 16 GB is the better default for frequent readers because it has the larger 7-inch 300 ppi display, adjustable warm light, waterproofing, and longer listed battery life. The Kindle 16 GB is better if compact size and lighter one-hand carry matter more.

Is the Kindle Paperwhite heavier than the Kindle?

Yes. Amazon lists the Kindle at 5.56 ounces and the Kindle Paperwhite at 7.4 ounces. The Paperwhite gives you a larger screen and waterproofing, but the smaller Kindle is easier to hold and pack.

Do both models have 16 GB of storage?

Yes. Amazon lists both models with 16 GB of on-device storage, which Amazon describes as holding thousands of books.

Which model is better for bath, pool, or beach reading?

Choose the Kindle Paperwhite. Amazon lists the Paperwhite as waterproof to IPX8, tested for immersion in 2 meters of fresh water for 60 minutes. The basic Kindle technical details checked for this page did not list that waterproofing.

Should you buy the basic Kindle instead?

Buy the Kindle 16 GB if you want the smallest and lightest current Kindle, read mostly in short sessions, and do not need waterproofing, a 7-inch screen, or warmer evening lighting.
