Chef Collection wins on surface and access; Victoria wins when the brief is a lighter, deeper 10-inch.

These two pans optimize opposite ends of skillet cooking. The Victoria is a deep, light, compact vessel — the pan for smaller households that still cook one-pan meals. The Chef Collection is a wide, shallow, open pan — built so a spatula can get under anything, for more people at once.

As a default, the Chef Collection's published numbers carry more everyday weight: an 11.81-inch cooking-surface length (the family's largest) on a 9.12-inch flat bottom, ergonomic handles, and dual pour spouts — a feature the Victoria's page does not claim at all, alongside the assist-handle gap noted there.

The Victoria's counter is honest and specific: a published 4.8 pounds (Lodge publishes no comparable weight for the Chef Collection here), a deeper vessel than the Chef's 2-inch profile, and flaxseed-oil seasoning. If your skillet life is small-batch and depth-first, it is the right pan. For most people choosing a main skillet, surface and access win.

Fit check

Choose by depth versus access.

Both pans cook dinner. The fit check is which physical quality your routine leans on: a deeper vessel that contains, or an open profile that lets tools in — at the size your household actually needs.

Buy Victoria 10-Inch if

You cook smaller batches but still want depth: braises, one-pan meals, and contained frying in a 10-inch pan, at a published 4.8 pounds that is easy to move and store. Its three-layer flaxseed-oil seasoning and exact published dimensions round out a very checkable spec sheet.

It covers gas, electric, ceramic, and induction stovetops plus oven, grill, and campfire use on its official page.

Quick reality check: there are no pour spouts or assist handle stated on Victoria's page, and 10 inches crowds a family batch — draining and two-handed moves are this pan's weak moments.

Buy Lodge Chef Collection 12-Inch if

You cook for a household and work the pan with a spatula: eggs, fish, sautes, and pancakes that reward a low entry angle. The Chef Collection's sloped sidewalls, 2-inch depth, and 11.81-inch cooking-surface length are published for exactly that, with ergonomic handles and dual pour spouts for draining.

It shares the 9.12-inch flat bottom of the Classic 12-inch, so burner fit is easy to check against Lodge's published footprint.

Quick reality check: the selected Amazon listing is labelled Everyday Pan (INLODGE.LC12EP) rather than LC12SK, and the shallower profile holds less than a deep skillet for saucy meals.

FAQ

Questions before checkout.

Which skillet is better for most people?

The Lodge Chef Collection 12-Inch. It publishes the largest cooking-surface length in this family (11.81 inches) with sloped, spatula-friendly sidewalls, ergonomic handles, and dual pour spouts. Choose the Victoria 10-Inch when you have settled on a lighter, deeper 10-inch pan — it publishes 4.8 pounds and a deeper-vessel design with flaxseed-oil seasoning.

Which pan is deeper?

The Victoria is the deeper design: it is described as a deeper vessel and stands 2.8 inches tall overall. The Chef Collection publishes a 2-inch cooking-surface depth with sloped sidewalls — its shape trades containment for access.

How much lighter is the Victoria?

Victoria publishes 4.8 pounds for its 10-inch skillet. Lodge does not publish a comparable weight for the Chef Collection 12-inch on the page used here, so no exact gap is stated — the Victoria is the smaller pan with the published light weight.

Why does the Chef Collection's Amazon listing say Everyday Pan?

The selected Amazon ASIN B087C97BF9 is titled Chef Collection Everyday Pan and lists model INLODGE.LC12EP. Lodge's own comparable skillet is the LC12SK. The listing is disclosed rather than silently treated as the identical retail configuration, so confirm the exact item at checkout.

Do both work on induction and in the oven?

Yes. Victoria lists gas, electric, ceramic, and induction stovetops plus oven, grill, and campfire use. Lodge lists the Chef Collection for any stovetop including induction, plus ovens, grills, and campfires. Follow the current product instructions for your exact item.
